# Bits + Bips — "What Iran, Oil Shocks, and No Rate Cuts Mean for Crypto"

**Host:** Steve Ehrlich (head of research, Sharplink). **Guest:** Lawrence Fralson (research analyst, Kaiko).

## TL;DR

- **Bitcoin liquidity has collapsed 40%** — market depth at 1% from mid price dropped from $25M to $15M since October 10th, with volumes down 30–40%, leaving BTC behaving more like a high-beta tech stock than an inflation hedge.
- **Open interest halved** — from $35B across the market six months ago to $15B now, a metric Fralson says must recover before BTC can credibly resume an uptrend above $72–74K.
- **Asia is the only buying session** — cumulative returns per session are +0.2% for Asia over the past three months, versus -11% to -12% for EU and US sessions.
- **Odds of zero Fed cuts in 2026 jumped from 5% to 33% in 48 hours** — CME options markets priced in persistent inflation fears after the Fed held rates and Powell signaled caution about energy-driven inflation.
- **Agentic commerce is accelerating** — Coinbase's X402 has processed $34M in volume; Tempo launched its mainnet; Google is entering with its own payments protocol; 2025 stablecoin volume hit $33T (almost entirely human-driven so far).
- **The CFTC/SEC joint interpretive guidance landed with a muted market reaction** — Fralson notes the move from 60K to 72–74K may have already priced it in; Ehrlich argues most guidance was already telegraphed and interpretive guidance carries no force of law.
- **Liberty Financial sold 6–12 hours before the October 10th crash** — Kaiko's research identified the pattern, raising governance concerns about Trump administration crypto activity.
- **Fralson is long-term bullish** — regulatory clarity building globally (Korea, Japan) and infrastructure investment make him "very optimistic" for the next few years, even if short-term conditions remain challenging.

**Bitcoin Liquidity and Market Structure**

Fralson describes a market structurally weakened since October 10th. Market depth at 1% from the mid price dropped from $25M to $15M on average, and volumes are down 30–40%. Open interest fell from $35B to $15B. This thinning of liquidity has amplified volatility — producing the sharp short-squeeze rally from 60K to 72–74K in recent weeks — but does not signal a healthy recovery. Fralson argues BTC is currently functioning as a high-beta tech stock rather than an inflation hedge, and that market depth, volumes, and open interest all need to recover before calling a durable bottom.

ETF flows turning positive after roughly three months of outflows helped stabilize BTC in the 60–70K range. A $2B options expiry with Max Pain at $70K looms in the near term. For longer-dated options, the market is pricing a binary outcome: heavy bets on $100K by December expiry, alongside significant put positioning targeting $40–50K.

**Geographic Flow Divergence and the Coinbase Premium**

Fralson highlights a shift in who is buying. Asia, which had been a consistent seller for much of the prior six months, flipped positive over the past three months with cumulative session returns of +0.2%. Meanwhile, EU and US sessions are down -11% to -12% cumulatively. Despite this Asian buying, Fralson notes the market remains heavily dependent on Coinbase flows: when the Coinbase spot premium over Binance appears — currently running at roughly 0.2%, or $50–$500 in dollar terms — BTC tends to move favorably, and traders actively monitor it for positioning signals.

**Macro: Fed Hold, Oil Shocks, and Financial Conditions**

All major central banks held rates during the week — the US, ECB, and Bank of England. The Australian central bank raised rates. Fralson argues that financial conditions matter more than headline Fed rates, and conditions have historically tightened long before official rate hikes begin. CME options markets moved odds of zero cuts in 2026 from 5% to 33% in 48 hours as oil prices rose. He expresses concern that US equity indices are "still pretending like nothing is happening," while European markets are already pricing in rate hike expectations — noting that oil shocks increase inflation and decrease growth simultaneously, leaving central banks without a clean policy response.

Ehrlich adds that US energy self-sufficiency makes domestic indices somewhat more resilient than European peers, but flags passive ETF inflows as a factor that can delay repricing. Both note concerns about private credit contagion, with Bank of America having recently walked back a memo recommending shorts on European stocks exposed to private equity and private credit.

**Agentic Commerce and Stablecoin Infrastructure**

Fralson frames agentic commerce as the most interesting structural development in crypto over the past three to six months. Coinbase's X402 payment standard — live on Ethereum and Solana, recently upgraded to support nearly any ERC-20 token beyond just USDC — has processed $34M in volume. Tempo launched its mainnet with a machine-to-machine payments protocol. Google announced its own payments protocol. The competitive landscape is fragmenting across permissionless blockchains and more centralized or permissioned alternatives.

Fralson argues the key enabling condition is robust, accurate pricing mechanisms: agents transacting at incorrect prices could generate outsized losses or gains for counterparties. He ties agentic payments closely to stablecoin growth, noting $33T in stablecoin volume in 2025 — almost entirely human-generated — as the baseline infrastructure that agentic commerce will build on.

Fralson and Ehrlich debate how to measure genuine adoption. Unique active addresses are imperfect because agentic wallets are often ephemeral. Fralson's approach at Kaiko involves on-chain monitoring focused on transaction directionality, counterparty clustering, and comparisons of unique address profiles across stablecoins — noting USDT and USDC show meaningfully different unique address counts and behavior.

**Security Risks in Agentic Payments**

Fralson highlights address poisoning as an underappreciated risk for AI agents making on-chain transactions. In this attack, a malicious actor sends a token to a victim from an address whose final characters match the victim's own address, but with different middle characters. AI agents have already been shown to be susceptible — mistakenly copying a poisoned address from transaction history instead of the intended recipient. He argues this and related attack vectors require additional security layers before agentic payment systems can be safely deployed at scale.

**CFTC/SEC Interpretive Guidance and Market Reaction**

The CFTC and SEC jointly issued interpretive guidance clarifying that major crypto assets are commodities rather than securities, and that NFTs and meme coins are not securities. Ehrlich argues the muted market reaction was predictable: most of what was covered had already been effectively resolved through prior litigation outcomes, enforcement withdrawals, and guidance issued under the new SEC leadership. He also notes that interpretive guidance is the lowest tier of SEC rulemaking — it carries no force of law and can be reversed by a future administration. The real milestone would be market structure legislation passing into law, which Senator Lummis suggested could clear committee by end of April.

Fralson acknowledges he expected a stronger positive reaction but concedes the earlier rally from 60K to 72–74K may have already incorporated the anticipated regulatory tailwind — a buy-the-rumor dynamic.

**Governance Concerns: Liberty Financial and the Trump Administration**

Fralson references Kaiko research finding that Liberty Financial — a Trump family-affiliated entity — began selling positions 6–12 hours before the October 10th market crash, which coincided with Trump's tariff announcement. He characterizes the Trump administration's crypto activity as operating in "a gray area, kind of a red area" from a regulatory and governance standpoint. Ehrlich flags unresolved issues in pending crypto legislation: the treatment of stablecoin yield, and whether the Trump family and close advisors should be permitted to participate in crypto markets while the administration is shaping crypto policy.

**Long-Term Outlook**

Fralson closes with a bullish long-term view. He points to regulatory momentum globally — Korea and Japan are actively working to integrate crypto into their financial systems — and to the infrastructure buildout underway across stablecoin chains, tokenized treasuries, and money market fund collateral as reasons for optimism over a multi-year horizon. His near-term read is more cautious: liquidity metrics need to recover, investor confidence needs to rebuild, and the geopolitical and macro backdrop remains uncertain.
