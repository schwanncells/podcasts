# Unchained — "How Microsoft Won the OpenAI Fight as Markets Rally on Iran"

**Host:** Chris Perkins (sub-hosting for Austin; managing director, CoinFund). **Guest:** Ram Ahlawalia (CEO, Lumida Wealth; macro and digital asset portfolio manager).

## TL;DR

- **Iran offered to stop Hormuz attacks in exchange for a full end to the war and postponement of nuclear talks** — the proposal, reported by the WSJ, includes lifting the U.S. blockade on Iranian ports but does not seek a broader sanctions lift; both hosts called the de-escalation trajectory "constructive."
- **Microsoft won the OpenAI restructuring deal decisively** — Microsoft drops ongoing revenue share payments to OpenAI, continues receiving payments from OpenAI through 2030 regardless of technical progress, retains IP license through 2032 (now non-exclusive), and holds a free call option on all future model R&D; Ahlawalia called it "more revenue, less expense, and a call option on the IP."
- **OpenAI breached its Microsoft contract by cutting the Amazon deal** — Ahlawalia argued that the breaching party has no negotiating leverage, which explains why Microsoft extracted such favorable terms; "Sam cut a deal that was illegal under contract law."
- **Bottom-up analyst consensus for S&P earnings growth is 19% year-over-year** — versus 12% last year; Ahlawalia called the number "bananas," citing acceleration across every GDP component: government capex, AI infrastructure spend, healthy consumer credit, and low jobless claims.
- **Anthropic's secondary market valuation has surpassed OpenAI's** — bid around $1 trillion among retail investors, despite starting later and with fewer resources; "Sam dropped the ball 10 ways to Sunday. He's birthed his own competition."
- **DeFi responded to the KelpDAO/LayerZero hack with cross-chain solidarity** — Avalanche, Solana, and other competitors joined recovery efforts; Perkins called it a market-driven solution but argued the industry still needs a policy response enabling private-sector offensive cyber operations.
- **Bitcoin/gold ratio is "beautiful" and Bitcoin dominance is still expanding** — both hosts constructive on crypto near-term but Ahlawalia cautioned against altcoin rotation: "It's going to be a pivot into fundamentals, not a pivot into alts."
- **Prediction markets are entering macro analysis** — Ahlawalia uses congressional election outcome markets to position in health care and solar stocks; views Polymarket vs. Kalshi as a Pepsi-Coke duopoly with liquidity determining the winner.

**Iran De-escalation and the Hormuz Blockade**

The WSJ reported that Iran presented a deal to lift the Hormuz blockade: stop attacks on ships in the strait in exchange for a full end to the war and a postponement of nuclear negotiations, without seeking a broader sanctions lift beyond restoring oil export flow. Ahlawalia noted the blockade is achieving its intended economic effect — Iran is being forced to use aging oil tankers as floating storage because its onshore capacity is full, and if it is forced to shut in production wells, recovery will be extremely difficult given Iran's reliance on water injection at natural pressure. "When you shut in a well, you're taking one that was once productive and putting it on the back burner." Both hosts noted Trump has political incentive to close the deal before the American bicentennial on July 4th and ahead of a planned meeting with Xi Jinping, creating a constructive setup for a resolution.

**Market Outlook: Bullish Signals Across the Board**

Ahlawalia laid out a broad bullish case: bottom-up S&P earnings growth consensus is 19% year-over-year — sharply above last year's already-strong 12% — driven by government spending, AI capex from hyperscalers and increasingly from the Pentagon, a healthy consumer (as confirmed by large bank, credit card issuer, and regional bank earnings), and low jobless claims. He expects the incoming Fed chair (Borsh) to be reluctant to raise rates, making real and financial assets attractive. A large pool of sidelined cash from investors who de-risked during the recent sell-off will provide a steady bid on dips. He sketched a potential outline: a rally from April through July correlating with tech/software, followed by some softness, and recovery toward year-end — though he emphasized this is subject to revision.

**Microsoft Wins the OpenAI Restructuring**

The deal as described: Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner through 2032; products ship first on Azure; Microsoft's IP license is non-exclusive going forward; Microsoft stops paying revenue share to OpenAI; OpenAI continues making revenue share payments to Microsoft through 2030 independently of its technical progress. Ahlawalia's framing was that when a startup breaches a contract with a large corporate, the breaching party loses negotiating leverage — and the Amazon cloud deal reportedly violated OpenAI's exclusivity agreement with Microsoft. As a result, Microsoft walked away with reduced expenses, continued upside on OpenAI's growth, and a call option on all future model R&D it didn't have to fund. "They had wins because the Microsoft Cloud would benefit from all of OpenAI's development... Microsoft gets a free call option on all of that." Ahlawalia described Microsoft as one of his top three positions and called it mispriced.

**OpenAI's Hardware Ambitions vs. Apple and Google**

Reports emerged of OpenAI working with two semiconductor companies and Johnny Ive on a phone or novel AI-first device. Both hosts were skeptical about challenging Apple: the switching costs out of Apple's ecosystem are enormous, Apple has no credible open AI App Store competitor, and both platforms will adapt to agentic AI on their own terms. Ahlawalia argued that the ideal near-term AI interface is already here — audio plus mobile plus headphones — and that the R2-D2 hologram form factor is still years away. "The best form factor, the most intuitive one, is just audio." He drew a parallel to the Solana phone, which has struggled to find product-market fit despite strong backing.

**Anthropic vs. OpenAI**

Ahlawalia noted that Anthropic — which spun out of OpenAI and was started later with fewer resources — now has a secondary market bid around $1 trillion, ahead of OpenAI's valuation, and that Claude has been rate-limiting due to demand while OpenAI is publicly saying it is "growth constrained" by compute. Multiple OpenAI spinouts (Dario Amodei's Anthropic, the former CTO's Thinking Machines, Mira Murati's Turing Post) now compete with the parent. "Sam has dropped the ball 10 ways to Sunday. He's birthed his own competition."

**Tech Earnings Week**

Microsoft, Google, Meta, and Amazon report Wednesday after the close; Apple reports Thursday. Ahlawalia's focus is on ROI evidence for AI capex — he expects it will be there, as it has been for the past two quarters. He is most interested in Microsoft (top three position, mispriced) and Meta (capex spending "like a drunken sailor" but funded by strong core business). He views Google's cloud business positively — 30% cloud growth — though its chipset (TPUs) is capacity-constrained because Nvidia locked down TSMC capacity. Amazon he considers on the expensive side post-rally. Apple he also views as expensive and says it faces ongoing AI leadership criticism, though he notes Apple historically "tends not to be first, but when they come to market they crush everybody else."

**Crypto: Fundamentals Over Alts**

Both hosts are constructive on crypto but with discipline. Ahlawalia's framework: Bitcoin is performing well relative to gold (Bitcoin/gold ratio showing a strong chart), Bitcoin dominance is still expanding, and the secular narrative has shifted from "AI apocalypse" to "we need more compute yesterday." He argued that institutional entry is changing the market structure permanently: rather than a rotation from Bitcoin into speculative altcoins at cycle peaks, institutions will pursue a "pivot into fundamentals," rewarding projects with real revenue and defensible differentiation. He singled out Bitcoin and Ethereum as differentiated by their trustless, permissionless architecture. He remains skeptical of meme coins and cautioned that crypto still competes with AI names and semiconductors for investor attention.

**KelpDAO/LayerZero Hack and DeFi Security**

Perkins described the hack as demonstrating the deep interconnectedness of DeFi and raised the question of how $127 trillion in equities markets can realistically migrate on-chain given persistent security vulnerabilities. He noted a positive market-driven response: competitors including Avalanche and Solana joined recovery efforts — "Now is not the time to burn everything to the ground" — and argued this cross-chain solidarity reflects the industry's maturity. However, he has called repeatedly for a policy response: empowering the private sector to conduct offensive cyber operations against state-sponsored hackers. "We need to hack them back. And we need to make it very painful for them." He attributed the escalating sophistication of hacks partly to social engineering that is difficult for humans to reliably resist, and argued that institutional capital waiting to enter crypto is blocked specifically by unresolved security concerns.

**Prediction Markets: Signal vs. Noise**

Ahlawalia has integrated prediction markets into macro analysis — specifically congressional election outcome contracts — to position in sectors like health care (Medicare Advantage) and solar that depend on which party controls Congress. He views Polymarket and Kalshi as a natural duopoly (Pepsi vs. Coke) with liquidity as the key moat; once concentrated, derivatives liquidity is sticky. He is skeptical that 1,000 sub-contracts are needed — he wants liquidity concentrated in a few high-signal categories (interest rates, policy decisions, major elections). Both hosts agreed regulated prediction markets are covered by existing anti-manipulation law, and that the special operations soldier who bet on a U.S. military raid faces more severe consequences than typical inside traders because operational security leaks can cost lives. Sports betting markets face political headwinds at the state level despite CFTC chair Selig's push for federal preemption.

**Macro Conviction: Post-Market-Depression Syndrome**

Ahlawalia closed with a contrarian observation: the recent 3–4 week rally — which he described as occurring in "0.6% of market history" in terms of magnitude — has not moved bull/bear sentiment surveys at all. The University of Michigan consumer sentiment index remains near lows. A New York Times piece reported 40% of Americans believe the apocalypse is imminent. He argued this extreme despondency is itself a bullish setup: "Markets aren't going to give people a chance to reenter." He also framed global decoupling as an unexpected stimulus: every sovereign economy is now investing materially to secure its own technological stack, which translates into elevated government spending worldwide — bullish for earnings in the medium term even if unhealthy long-term. The S&P falling only 9% despite the Hormuz blockade, which he called "probably one of the most significant geopolitical events one could imagine," signals underlying economic resilience.
