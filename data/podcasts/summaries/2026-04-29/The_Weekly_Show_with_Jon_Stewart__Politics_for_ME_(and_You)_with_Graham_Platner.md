# The Daily Show with Michael Costa — "Politics for ME (and You)"

**Host:** Michael Costa. **Guests:** Graham Platner (Democratic Maine Senate candidate); Paul Gracias (Republican nominee for Office of Special Counsel); Ronnie Chang (Daily Show correspondent).

## TL;DR

- **Trump's White House ballroom construction demolished the East Wing** — the 90,000 square-foot ballroom project that promised not to "touch" the White House proceeded to demolish the East Wing, which houses the First Lady's office.
- **Paul Gracias, nominated to lead the Office of Special Counsel, made Nazi references in leaked group chats** — text messages allegedly showed him writing "I do have a Nazi streak in me from time to time" and proposing to consign Martin Luther King Jr. Day "to the seventh circle of hell where it belongs."
- **Graham Platner, Maine Senate candidate, made racist Reddit posts deleted ahead of campaign launch** — posts from five years prior questioned why black customers don't tip while also calling himself a communist and dismissing rural white Americans as "racist and stupid."
- **Platner acknowledged his mistake and credited structural injustice** — when asked about the "don't tip" comment, he attributed the stereotype to a lack of agency caused by structural injustice after a conversation with a black bartender friend.
- **Gracias deflected by claiming AI authentication is unreliable** — his attorney's response stated "in this age of AI, authentication of allegedly leaked messages is extremely difficult," rather than denying or apologizing for the content.
- **Costa and Ronnie Chang satirize competing narratives on racism** — the segment shifts to absurdist comedy where Chang argues racism existed before documentation, and they explore how one's visibility determines political consequences.

**Trump's White House Ballroom and East Wing Demolition**

Costa opens with Trump's plan to construct a 90,000 square-foot ballroom at the White House. Trump defended the project by stating it would not "interfere with the current building" and would "pay total respect to the existing building." However, demolition crews began tearing down walls in the East Wing almost immediately—the same wing that houses the First Lady's office. Costa sardonically notes the irony that the promised non-invasive renovation resulted in demolishing the very structure it claimed to protect, joking that the general contractor must be "Bin Laden and sons."

**The Paul Gracias Nazi Text Scandal**

Costa pivots to a Republican controversy. Members of the Young Republicans group had their texts leak, allegedly containing jokes about gas chambers and praise for Hitler. Paul Gracias, nominated to lead the Office of Special Counsel, became the focus when similar offensive messages emerged from his group chat. Gracias reportedly wrote that he has "a Nazi streak in me from time to time," and in another message said Martin Luther King Jr. Day "should be ended and tossed into the seventh circle of hell." Other leaked texts allegedly included the statement "Never trust a China or Indian. Never" and offensive characterizations of Black History Month. Costa notes sardonically that when someone's racist remarks are so extreme that other members of the racist group chat respond with shock ("Jesus Christ. Wow."), that person has crossed a line. When reached for comment, Gracias's attorney claimed that "in this age of AI, authentication of allegedly leaked messages is extremely difficult"—a non-denial that merely asserts AI makes proof difficult.

**Graham Platner's Old Reddit Posts and Structural Racism**

Costa turns to Graham Platner, a Democrat running for Senate in Maine, who faced scrutiny after CNN reviewed old Reddit posts he made under the handle "5 years ago." The posts, deleted ahead of his campaign launch, included the claim that he couldn't understand why black customers didn't tip while bartending, along with statements calling himself a communist, dismissing police as "bastards," and describing rural white Americans as "racist and stupid." Costa invites Platner to respond directly. Platner acknowledges that he once held the stereotypical view about tipping but says a conversation with a black bartender friend changed his perspective. The bartender explained structural injustice and the role of lack of agency in shaping behavior—a framing that helped Platner understand the stereotype wasn't about inherent traits but systemic conditions. Costa gives the apology a "B minus," noting that Platner at least acknowledged the mistake and learned, unlike Gracias's deflection.

**Contrasting Responses and the Politics of Receipts**

Costa and correspondent Ronnie Chang discuss the divergent fates of the two scandals. Chang notes that Gracias, being Republican, likely saw his approval rating improve—a dark comedic observation on the political incentives at play. For Platner in Maine, Chang jokes that residents have "bigger problems" (implying the heroin crisis) and may not care. Chang then pivots to satirizing the broader conversation about racism and documentation, suggesting that racism existed before the internet created paper trails, and therefore people could quietly express racist views without consequence. This launches into an extended comedic bit where Costa and Chang trade deliberately racist statements about Asian, white, and other groups, each attributing their own remarks to AI or "structural injustice," ultimately illustrating how absurd and malleable the explanations become when divorced from genuine accountability.

**Notes on Response and Accountability**

The episode juxtaposes two approaches to scandal: Platner's acknowledgment of his past racism paired with an explanation rooted in systemic factors, and Gracias's non-denial that obscures the actual content by questioning whether the messages are even real. Costa's framing suggests the former is at least honest engagement with past mistakes, while the latter is evasion. The comedic coda with Ronnie Chang satirizes how hard it has become to differentiate sincere growth from performative apology when the language of structural injustice can be weaponized to excuse any statement.
