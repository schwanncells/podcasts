# Unchained — "Bitcoin's Price May Have Bottomed. One Reason Is the AI Debasement Trade"

**Host:** Laura Shin. **Guest:** Alex Thorne (head of firm-wide research, Galaxy; veteran crypto researcher formerly at Fidelity).

## TL;DR

- **Bitcoin bottomed in late June 2025** — the 50-week moving average has historically marked the ceiling of bear markets and the floor of bull markets; Bitcoin has now held above this level for five or six consecutive weeks, and every prior instance of this pattern (three to four bear markets) preceded a bull market with zero further lower bottoms.
- **The bear market is the shallowest on record because Bitcoin's ownership has matured** — cycles are compressing in magnitude due to asset maturity; Bitcoin ETFs have brought long-term institutional capital that historically buffers bear markets; macro hedge funds reduced positions while wealth managers and sovereign wealth funds added in Q2 2026.
- **Old coin awakening (likely sales) is fading sharply** — dormant Bitcoin holders moving coins to exchanges fell from 67,000 BTC per month in 2024 to 47,000 in 2025 to 34,000 in 2026, suggesting the "IPO moment" of early holders taking gains is largely complete and sell pressure is easing.
- **U.S. national debt has doubled from $23 trillion (2020) to $40 trillion (2026), strengthening the debasement thesis for Bitcoin** — the monetary conditions that motivated the Bitcoin white paper in 2009 have only worsened; AI buildout is funded by private sector credit expansion and tax incentives, both forms of debasement; bailout risk means debasement either way.
- **Surveillance capacity has grown exponentially, making censorship-resistant money increasingly relevant** — TVs, phones, and AI agents now enable continuous monitoring; AI agents are highly capable with public blockchains and could facilitate both on-chain DeFi and peer-to-peer transactions; Bitcoin offers a credible exit valve from surveillance.
- **Institutional adoption is shifting from permissioned to permissionless blockchains** — while tokenization and real-world assets dominated Korea Blockchain Week and Token Summit 2026, this cyclical focus away from native assets historically preceded rallies; permissioned blockchains (JPM's Quorum, Connex; Digital Asset's Canton) have repeatedly failed to deliver meaningful adoption, and the shift back to Bitcoin and Ether is a contrarian positioning opportunity.
- **Tripwires that would invalidate the thesis: failure to hold the 50-week moving average (currently ~75K), cryptographically relevant quantum computers, major AI-driven cryptographic breaks, or a severe market shock similar to March 2020** — current price ~80K sits marginally above critical support; even severe economic downturns create bailout/money-printing scenarios that ultimately favor Bitcoin.
- **Thorne's conservative price target is Bitcoin returning to its prior all-time high of ~125K; he made a 250K end-of-2027 prediction a year ago but emphasizes higher long-term potential without updating that target.**

---

**Four Pillars of a Bitcoin Bottom**

Thorne's thesis rests on four pillars: technical analysis via moving averages, fundamental debasement, positioning data, and sentiment. On technicals, the 200-week moving average has historically set the floor of bear markets and the 50-week moving average has marked the ceiling. When Bitcoin closes above the 50-week for multiple consecutive weeks and turns it into support—a pattern that occurred during three to four prior bear markets—a bull market has followed with zero further bottoms. The current five to six weeks of closes above the 50-week level aligns with this historical pattern, suggesting the June 2025 low was the bear market bottom.

Thorne notes that the current bear market was the shallowest on record, and the prior peak in October 2025 was also the shallowest; over Bitcoin's 17-year history, cycles are compressing in magnitude. Using logarithmic price analysis, the curve shows decay rather than parabolic growth, suggesting an S-shaped pattern as the asset matures. An asset worth $10 million can achieve 1,000x gains more easily than a $1 trillion asset. Bitcoin's volatility has declined substantially, and it now attracts longer-term capital—wealth managers, registered investment advisors, and sovereign wealth funds—rather than fast-moving speculation.

**Positioning: From Macro Hedge Funds to Institutional Wealth Managers**

The ownership composition has shifted in ways that reduce downside pressure. In Q2 2026, CFTC data showed that asset manager net long positions and leverage trader net short positions both declined substantially. Basis traders—who typically short futures while holding spot—have largely exited because treasuries and money market funds now offer equal or better returns with no complexity. Examining SEC 13F filings for Bitcoin spot ETF ownership, Thorne found macro and multi-strat hedge funds (Brevin, Millennium, Capula, DE Shaw) reducing or exiting entirely, while Morgan Stanley Wealth Management, Mubadala, and Abu Dhabi's sovereign wealth funds were among the largest adders. The practical implication: people panicking about AI trade volatility do not own Bitcoin, so they cannot sell it in a panic. Wealth managers rebalance on a quarterly or longer basis, making for slower-moving, more stable capital. This positioning lightness means an opportunity to buy without fighting momentum.

**Old Coin Awakening Is Fading**

Another signal of reduced sell pressure comes from on-chain dormant coin activity. In 2024, long-dormant Bitcoin holders moved 67,000 BTC per month onto exchanges; in 2025, that fell to 47,000 per month; and in 2026 (January through September), it averaged just 34,000 per month. While moving coins does not always mean selling (custody rotations, hardware wallet upgrades), at scale the heuristic holds. Thorne calls this Bitcoin's "IPO moment," when early holders need to take gains as the asset matures. This redistribution is healthy and necessary—it transfers supply from early believers to newer entrants bullish at higher prices, setting a higher floor. But the diminishing trend in 2026 shows this pressure is substantially eased, and Thorne expects 2026 full-year awakening to be less than half of 2025, which was already down from 2024.

**Debasement: The Stronger Case**

The macroeconomic backdrop for Bitcoin has only worsened since Satoshi published the white paper in 2009. U.S. national debt was roughly $23 trillion in 2020; it is now $40 trillion. The interest paid on that debt has grown by roughly an order of magnitude over the same span. Even since the October 2025 all-time high, debasement has intensified. Bond traders have abandoned any pretense of disbelief; they openly acknowledge the debasement trade. Government stimulus expectations aside, the AI buildout itself is a vector for debasement: it is funded by massive private-sector credit expansion (effectively private money printing) and massive tax incentives that subsidize treasury spending. If AI scaling succeeds, growth might offset some monetary expansion. If it falters, Thorne believes government bailouts are unavoidable because the entire economy—retirement accounts, governments, all major markets—is "so tied up in this trade." Either way, money printing follows. The likelihood of "astronomical money printing" has only increased, buttressing Bitcoin's value proposition as a fixed-supply hedge against debasement.

**Surveillance, AI Agents, and Censorship-Resistant Money**

Thorne's essay includes a provocative claim: "The need for money that no one can freeze, surveil or reprogram grows with the capacity to do those things. AI has made that capacity cheap." He elaborates that tracking technology—facial recognition, audio recording in consumer electronics, bandwidth-intensive AI agents—has made mass surveillance feasible in ways it was not even a year ago. China's face-scanning infrastructure could not track every person; modern AI agents can. This dystopian scenario motivates interest in censorship-resistant currency. While Bitcoin may not be programmable for DeFi like Ether, and while AI agents may prefer stablecoins for simple transactions, the long-term case for exit valves from surveillance states remains compelling. Thorne notes that frontier AI models (including Claude) are trained on GitHub and public code, making them highly capable at analyzing blockchain transactions. Autonomous agents will use on-chain DeFi; whether they prefer Bitcoin, Ether, or stablecoins for privacy and censorship resistance is an open question, but demand for such tools is rising.

**The Tokenization Narrative and the Pendulum Effect**

A key contrarian observation: during the 2014–2016 and 2018–2019 bear markets, traditional finance repeatedly dismissed Bitcoin and crypto in favor of "blockchain technology" and permissioned ledgers. JPMorgan built Quorum, then later Connex (formerly Onyx); Digital Asset deployed Canton; enterprises formed countless blockchain consortiums. None of these meaningfully changed anyone's life. Yet each time this cycle occurred, buying Bitcoin and Ether proved prescient. The same pattern is repeating now: Korea Blockchain Week and Token Summit 2026 featured hundreds of panels on tokenization and real-world assets (RWAs), while Thorne was on "basically the only panel at KBW about Bitcoin." Institutional adoption of on-chain settlement and tokenized assets will likely happen this cycle, but Thorne's point is that this focus is cyclical and that Satoshi's core insight—"the root problem with traditional currency is all the trust required"—is separate from and more fundamental than the efficiency gains of faster settlement. Permissioned blockchains failed before; the sentiment shift suggests a historic opportunity to bet on the contrarian trade: native assets when everyone is bullish on infrastructure and tokenization.

**Risks and Tripwires**

Thorne identifies several scenarios that would invalidate his thesis. First, a meaningful and sustained break below the 50-week moving average (currently around 75K) would undermine the technical case for a bottom. Second, cryptographically relevant quantum computers, while not an immediate threat, could break Bitcoin's elliptic curve cryptography; the qubit requirement remains high but is declining. Third, AI-driven discoveries that weaken cryptographic assumptions pose a novel tail risk. Fourth, catastrophic AI-related cyberattacks or hacks could materially affect all markets. Fifth, a major equity correction—a true 20%+ decline—could spillover, though Thorne argues this is unlikely given that Bitcoin's recent move from 58K to 80K was driven by long-term holders, not equity traders. The March 2020 COVID shock is his historical reference point: Bitcoin fell 50% in one day. A similar sudden shock would reset the analysis. That said, he finds such a scenario unlikely given that recent AI market volatility has already subsided (SK Hynix reported well, Oracle's investor day was positive), and he frames ongoing AI anxiety as cyclical rather than permanent. Even in severe downturns, the bailout scenario creates debasement that favors Bitcoin—a catch-22 in his favor.

**Modest Price Targets**

Thorne avoids precise price projections, noting that options markets priced equal likelihood of 50K or 150K Bitcoin by end-2026, a range too wide to be useful. At the time of his prediction (end of last year with Bitcoin at 110K), he chose not to predict the path because of chaos; Bitcoin dipped to 58K, validating his caution. For 2027, he mentioned 250K as a plausible target made a year ago but has not updated it. His conservative read is that Bitcoin simply returns to its prior all-time high of approximately 125K, which he calls "pretty conservative" and frames as lower-bound. He emphasizes longer-term bullish positioning without specificity beyond these guideposts.

---

**Conclusion**

Thorne assembles a multi-angle case for Bitcoin accumulation: technical patterns suggest the bear market is over; positioning is light and skewed toward long-term holders; debasement has worsened since the white paper; old-holder capitulation is fading; institutional infrastructure is maturing; and sentiment has swung so far toward tokenization and infrastructure that a contrarian bet on the native asset is historically opportune. He acknowledges drawdown risk and tail scenarios but frames them as unlikely or ultimately self-reinforcing (bailouts → debasement → Bitcoin). The near-term signal to watch is sustained closes above the 50-week moving average; breaching that would require reassessment. Beyond that, Thorne positions the current environment as a rare moment to hold or add to Bitcoin with favorable risk-adjusted entry before long-term institutional capital awakens to the debasement thesis and the positioning re-equilibrates.
