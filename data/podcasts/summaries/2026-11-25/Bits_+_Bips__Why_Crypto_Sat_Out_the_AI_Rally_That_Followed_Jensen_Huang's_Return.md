# Bits + Bips — "Why Crypto Sat Out the AI Rally That Followed Jensen Huang's Return"

**Host:** Austin Campbell (Zero Knowledge Group). **Guests:** Ram Ahluwalia (wealth manager and investor; leads a firm the auto-transcript renders as "Luvino") and Chris Perkins (crypto investor; firm rendered in the transcript as "Franklin crypto"). Credentials are as heard in the auto-generated transcript.

## TL;DR

- **Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO** — less than two weeks after he resigned over an affair with a woman U.S. officials suspected of being a Chinese spy; Trump told CNBC "I convinced them," and the stock rallied about 30% (roughly $1 trillion of market cap).
- **The administration "played the national security card"** — by declaring no security risk, it gave the board a glide path to reverse course; the SEC probe continues, Huang is due to testify December 3rd, and a Democratic Congress in January is seen as the bigger risk than DOJ or SEC.
- **The AI trade is politically sorting** — Anthropic is seen as "team blue" and Nvidia as "team red"; Ahluwalia calls Huang's $25 million donation "the cheapest put option anyone ever bought."
- **Anthropic's IPO priced at $83 and raised about $93.5 billion** — the largest on record, with $400 billion of institutional demand; it opened at $111.50, closed day one up 17%, then slid back toward the mid-$80s.
- **Crypto lagged the relief rally** — Nvidia was up 33.5% intraday, the S&P over 5% and the Nasdaq over 7.5%, while Bitcoin traded sideways; panelists cite security overhangs, AI absorbing attention and capital, and a Democratic Congress.
- **Security is the dominant backdrop** — outages hit Microsoft, the FAA, major banks, Apple, the NSA, United and Delta over about 10 days; Perkins says the internet needs a rewrite with post-quantum cryptography, and sees crypto engineers as best placed to lead it.
- **Fed outlook** — the panel expects a 25 bp move in December, with Ahluwalia arguing the rally is equivalent to a 50 bp cut in liquidity terms.

**Huang's Return and the Market Reaction**

Campbell recounts that Nvidia's board reinstated Huang as president, CEO and director effective immediately, with the CFO returning to CFO duties only. Nvidia added a new export control committee and said it was confident the reinstatement poses no national security risk. Trump said "sex sells" and "I was represented at the board." Ahluwalia says the market "loves Jensen," pointing to a 30% Nvidia rally, guidance raised from 70% to 75%, memory names up 15% to 18%, and the view that the AI correction is over. He also notes the earlier reversals by the interim CEO and Treasury Secretary Bessent, who had said Nvidia needed an outsider and then that Huang was "on the outside."

Perkins calls Huang the "central banker of AI," arguing his return restores liquidity, including credit support agreements, to neoclouds and the open-weight model ecosystem. He argues the administration's national security determination was the decisive change, since "the one thing that always trumps innovation is national security." Campbell asks whether Trump contacted the DOJ and whether the board's own investigation will continue. He is not worried about DOJ or SEC under the current administration, but is concerned about Democrats in Congress, who Trump does not control. Ahluwalia adds that government officials are staffing up for subpoenas and information requests, and Perkins notes the statute of limitations outlasts the administration, which creates a different risk posture for a commercial enterprise.

**Politics, the Board and the AI Policy Framework**

The panel discusses companies picking sides. Ahluwalia says Anthropic looks aligned with Democrats and is "a loser from all of this," as are AMD and Broadcom, which rallied less. Campbell notes Zuckerberg has stayed largely silent. Campbell is sharply critical of Nvidia's board, saying it either moved too fast and had to reverse or waffled and lost credibility, and doubts most of the board or the CFO survive. Perkins responds that boards must operate as fiduciaries and that a changed national security assessment legitimately altered the calculus, though affairs with a client or employee remain serious.

Ahluwalia sees a bipartisan AI framework emerging after the data center backlash proved an election sideshow ("10,000 data centers, 99.999% of people never noticed"). He describes the Democratic framework as "single ply toilet paper," essentially "invest in your community and bring your own energy." The panel ties the centrist drift to affordability: Campbell argues voters twice in a row punished parties that promised normalcy and delivered inflation. They cite Texas's governor demanding data centers not raise local energy prices and Loudoun County's $1.3 billion in tax revenue. Campbell cautions that any licensing framework depends on who licenses whom, drawing on his experience with Operation Chokepoint 2.0.

**Frontier Labs, Competition and Valuations**

On OpenAI, Ahluwalia says frontier labs are trying to wean off Nvidia, with OpenAI developing a chip with Broadcom, but that they benefit net from Huang fueling the AI cycle. Campbell notes labs are on a treadmill of building new models and burning capital with weak pricing power, and that Google's new model release is crushing benchmarks, so it is "no longer a two person race." Ahluwalia argues leaders can still charge top prices, while the long tail will struggle to fund investment. He cites Dell raising prices 20% on memory costs. Nvidia's forward P/E was 8.8 at Friday's close, with the VIX at 40, which he calls the bottom and a "clearing event"; he expects markets up 10% to 15% next year on earnings growth and disinflation.

**Anthropic's IPO and Where Liquidity Goes**

Campbell reviews the Anthropic IPO: 1.127 billion shares at $83 plus a $26 billion placement to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC, with Nasdaq 100 entry slated for December 21. Perkins expects liquidity returning to early investors and employees to recycle into frontier AI startups, and possibly into crypto and quantum. Ahluwalia says the money will "go back to AI," and jokes it will go into San Francisco real estate, because people invest where they made their wealth. He argues crypto is an attention asset, and the attention and memes are around AI and Huang.

**Crypto's Case and the Token Debate**

Perkins pushes back, saying valuations in crypto are reasonable, regulatory clarity has improved, and Japanese institutions are moving methodically (legislation in 2027, tax fixes in 2028). He notes only 0.1% of roughly $160 trillion each in equity and fixed income markets is tokenized, and argues crypto benefits when it stops being sexy and business people drive infrastructure. Ahluwalia is frustrated, saying venture and founder focus has produced "cotton candy, sugar rush" and that "token's not worth anything" outside a narrow few. Perkins counters that a token is a wrapper with utility: 24/7 markets, composability and instant settlement. Campbell argues fintech is the hardest area for VCs because network effects work differently and the backend transformation of banks and the Federal Reserve is not VC-investable. He estimates crypto is two to three years from capitalizing on its opportunities, citing a CoinDesk and Inca Digital report on North Korean funds moving through Sky, where omnibus accounts and no pool-level freezing leave only a binary choice. Ahluwalia also notes that Crusoe and bitcoin miners have pivoted to AI.

**Security Incidents and the Post-Quantum Problem**

Campbell lists the outages across Microsoft, the FAA, banks, Apple, the NSA and airlines, noting officials such as Jay Clayton say they were not coordinated cyberattacks and Bessent likens them to a vaccine. Ahluwalia cites 170-plus hacks of large companies and expects cybersecurity stocks to be topping out, including after the Palo Alto Networks and Zscaler deal. Perkins says AI helps defenders but also attackers, forcing rushed patches that overwhelm systems, and that "the public key cryptography that protects our systems today is simply not good enough." He argues crypto, with its concentration of cryptographers and layer ones racing toward post-quantum compliance, will lead the rewrite. Campbell predicts a barbell in which companies are either surface-area minimized or true security professionals, with the middle "trucked" by AI.

**Fed Outlook**

Asked whether the Fed should still hike in December, Ahluwalia says the answer was no on Friday, but now $2 trillion of market cap created is "as if the Fed cut 50 bips," because liquidity is back. Perkins notes forward inflation indicators point to about 2.6% in a year, and cites Druckenmiller's remarks on the new Fed chair Warsh's dislike of quantitative easing. Campbell expects a 25 bp move, saying the Fed will keep chipping away at inflation while the market is not on fire.
