**Market Conditions: A Prolonged Losing Streak**

The S&P 500 closed the week at roughly 6,368 after five consecutive weekly declines — the longest losing streak since 2022. The Dow was down 1.7%, the NASDAQ has fallen more than 10% from its October peak, and the S&P is down approximately 7% year to date. The VIX remains elevated, and recession models are moving materially: Moody's is now pricing a 50% probability, Goldman Sachs has raised its odds to 30%, and Wilmington Trust and others are revising upward.

**Why This Is Worse Than Liberation Day**

Ram argues the current correction is more serious than the Liberation Day selloff because the new headwinds are structurally outside the control of monetary policy. Oil prices have surged due to conflict near the Strait of Hormuz, and 53% of S&P market cap is directly exposed to oil price levels. Agricultural commodities — wheat, corn — are also rising, adding inflationary pressure at the consumer level. "You can change interest rates, but that does not create more oil." Ram summarizes the backdrop bluntly: "war is highly inflationary."

Ram targets a 6,000–6,200 range on the S&P as the downside scenario — a roughly 15% correction that would retrace valuations to Liberation Day lows. He sees continued institutional and retail deleveraging, with market rallies consistently getting sold off as participants use gap-ups to degross positions. "People are being trained to sell rips," he notes. He also calls out complacency on social media, where retail investors are being talked into buying names down 40%–50% using sentiment indicators — fear and greed gauges — that he argues "don't have any relevance given the broader backdrop and structural forces and facts around the Strait of Hormuz."

**Investment Positioning: Stay Small, Selective Value**

Ram's primary posture is risk-off: hold excess cash, stay small, and avoid being a hero. He sees continued deleveraging from both institutions and retail. That said, he identifies pockets of emerging value and made three specific purchases: Berkshire Hathaway at 1.3x price-to-tangible-book, Meta at 17.5x forward PE, and Microsoft. He frames these as a "flight to fundamentals" rather than broad exposure — the right companies with the right fundamentals present a decent setup when sentiment is depressed.

The host asks whether this is the moment AI hyperscalers start to feel the cost of heavy CapEx spending and suspended buybacks. Ram largely agrees — the Mag Seven took their lumps earlier in the selloff, and while selective value exists within that group, it is not a category-wide opportunity. Consumer discretionary names are also under pressure as consumer spending pulls back.

**Sectors Under Pressure: Industrials, Biotech, and Software**

Ram identifies the industrials complex as a bubble that predated the current conflict and is now being exposed. He holds active short positions on names like Caterpillar, John Deere, and Caradine, calling the entire category "a bubble." Former market leaders with the highest relative strength have already rolled over — Micron, Lam Research, Bloom Energy among them. Biotech is also declining. Emerging markets are being hit by a stronger dollar.

In software, companies like MongoDB, Datadog, and ARM are still trading at 50–80x earnings, making it difficult to find names that can rise above sector-wide pressure from interest rates compressing multiples. Netflix he describes as still expensive; Tesla as egregiously overpriced. The environment is "caustic" for risk assets broadly.

**Bitcoin at $66K: Resilience With an Asterisk**

Bitcoin has held up notably well relative to the broader market selloff, trading sideways around $66K while equities have declined. Ram attributes this resilience almost entirely to STRC capital flows — Michael Saylor recently launched a new product that is being heavily marketed, and new clients are routing capital into Bitcoin through that product. Ram explicitly frames it as "not a healthy bid" capable of sustaining a broader crypto rally. He also notes that both gold and oil are currently absorbing significant liquidity that would otherwise support risk assets, making crypto's stability more noteworthy — but technically fragile rather than fundamentally driven.

**Crypto Fundamentals and the Regulatory Tailwind**

Despite near-term macro pressure, Ram describes the underlying crypto regulatory environment as the best he has seen. He attended the Digital Asset Summit the prior week, which he says had record registrations — notably from institutional investors new to the space who care about fundamentals. He views this as a structural sign that the asset class has demonstrated staying power.

The Clarity Act is identified as a potential major catalyst. If passed, Ram says it would be "a very, very nice tailwind for the asset class." His conclusion: when the current macro headwinds lift, crypto is well-positioned to benefit from both the improving regulatory framework and the institutional interest that has been quietly building behind the scenes.
