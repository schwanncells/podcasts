# Unchained — "Why Bitwise's CIO Says the Blue Wave Won't Derail Crypto's Bull Market"

**Host:** Laura Shin. **Guest:** Matt Hogan (Chief Investment Officer, Bitwise; crypto institutional strategist and analyst; leads crypto adoption work for major financial institutions).

## TL;DR

- **Bitwise projects Bitcoin to $1.6 million by 2036** — based on store-of-value market growth from $30T to $90T and Bitcoin capturing 25% (up from 5% today); outpaces JP Morgan's more conservative $1M projection.
- **Crypto bottomed in June 2026, early bull market underway** — despite major hacks, Democratic "blue wave," and recession fears, Bitcoin and crypto assets have remained resilient; institutional adoption by Wells Fargo, Morgan Stanley, JP Morgan driving flows.
- **Blue wave unlikely to derail crypto** — Democrats may investigate ethics violations (World Liberty Financial, Trump coin) and tighten DeFi rules, but crypto already has Gensler Act approval, pro-crypto SEC/CFTC, and token revenue rights; institutional adoption creates momentum that will be "too big to fail" within two years.
- **AI bubble scenarios all lead to Bitcoin upside** — whether AI creates abundance (scarce assets like Bitcoin win), causes recession (government stimulus helps), or muddles along (institutional adoption thesis holds), Bitcoin only loses if there's sharp recession with zero government response.
- **Institutions adopt through "rule of eight"** — average client needs eight meetings over two years before accepting crypto; explains why pension funds and sovereign wealth funds are just now coming online despite ETFs launching 2.5 years ago.
- **Hacks and quantum threats overblown for institutions** — institutional customers use ETFs and cold storage (never impacted); pre-2015 Bitcoin coins (Satoshi era) face quantum/AI decryption risk and are "one thing that keeps me up at night," but won't fundamentally change Bitcoin's long-term thesis.
- **Crypto-AI intersection still experimental** — Venice and Near show traction, but Pearl and other "proof of useful work" tokens are early-stage hype; real growth will be in stablecoins and tokenization, not AI tokens, over next 2–3 years.
- **Tokenized stocks adoption will come from edges first** — retail US investors don't need them (Schwab accounts work fine), but unbanked internationals and 24/7 hedge fund traders drive initial growth; Avalanche/NISEY deal legitimizes the use case without requiring major company adoption yet.

**The Early Bull Market and Resilience Through Headwinds**

Hogan argues crypto has entered the early stages of a multi-year bull market, having bottomed in June 2026. What stands out to him is how resilient crypto has remained despite a cascade of negative events: the $6.3 billion Binance hack (largest in history), Democratic electoral victory (bad for Clarity Act odds), rising inflation expectations, and Fed rate-hike chatter. He frames early bull markets as periods when bad news stops mattering—crypto is in that phase now. He expresses minor pessimism for the next few weeks due to uncertainty around Democratic direction on crypto, but projects a good year-end and strong long-term outlook.

**Three AI Scenarios All Lead to Bitcoin Wins**

Hogan dismisses concerns that an AI bubble could crash crypto. He maps three scenarios. In an AI utopia of infinite abundance, scarce assets like Bitcoin and coastal real estate "do exceptionally well." In a Democratic crackdown on AI (Anthropic priced below a trillion, neoclauds collapse, 2–4% GDP shock), Congress steps in with stimulus that benefits Bitcoin. In a muddle-along scenario, institutional adoption thesis holds unchanged. The only losing scenario—sharp recession with zero government response—he considers unlikely. This "heads we win, tails we win" framing echoes the analysis of risk-on/risk-off bifurcation in macro markets.

**The Democratic Blue Wave: Threat or Neutral?**

Hogan expects Democrats to investigate ethics violations (World Liberty Financial, Trump coin) and strengthen DeFi enforcement via BRCA language, targeting DeFi specifically. However, he argues this won't derail crypto because the industry has already secured the Gensler Act, a pro-crypto SEC, a pro-crypto CFTC, and regulatory clarity on token revenue generation. Within two years, if stablecoins and tokenization prove their utility and institutions adopt widely, crypto becomes "too big to fail" and resistant to policy reversal. He notes that two years ago, none of these wins existed—crypto moves fast. A vindictive Democratic effort is the downside risk, but he prices it as unlikely to overcome the momentum already in place.

**The Rule of Eight: Why Institutions Are Just Now Coming Online**

Hogan shares a principle he calls the "rule of eight": Bitwise's data shows that institutional clients typically require eight meetings—roughly one per quarter over two years—before they accept crypto as an asset class. This timeline mirrors ETF launches (2.5 years ago) and explains why the largest wealth management platforms (Wells Fargo, JP Morgan, Morgan Stanley) are only now meaningfully allocating to Bitcoin, and pension/sovereign wealth funds are just beginning conversations. It is not about crypto itself improving; it is about the *passage of time* normalizing the unfamiliar. He draws parallels to how ETFs, index funds, and private equity were all adopted—not because they suddenly became better, but because familiarity bred acceptance. He is currently addressing $3 trillion of wealth in a typical week, a dramatic shift from individual wealth managers five years ago.

**Institutions and Security: Cold Storage and Unbothered Hacks**

Contrary to expectations, institutions have not asked Hogan extensively about the numerous 2026 hacks. The reason: they underwrite crypto through ETFs and institutional custodians with true cold storage, a setup with an "exceptional track record." Exchange hacks are immaterial to them. Historically, the 10 largest hacks in Bitcoin's history show no predictive price impact over 30 days (five hacks followed by price rises, five by declines). Crypto retail customers (those with assets on exchanges or self-custody) absorb the damage; institutions do not.

Quantum and AI cryptography threats are mostly migration risks, not meltdowns. However, Satoshi-era coins (pre-2015) are uniquely vulnerable to quantum or AI decryption. Hogan acknowledges this "keeps me up at night" and expects "a very challenging community discussion soon," but he remains optimistic the community will find a solution. It is on his monitoring radar as "some smoke," though not a "nine alarm fire."

**Crypto-AI Intersection: Cambrian Explosion, Not Yet Mainstream**

Hogan sees the crypto-AI convergence as an experimental phase with a "Cambrian explosion" of small projects. Investors are eager to find intersection plays, and tokens like Venice have seen traction. However, most AI-crypto projects lack proven product-market fit. Bitwise publicly favors Near (AI overlay as "free rider" on solid blockchain fundamentals) and Venice (organic end-user demand visible in growth), but Pearl and other "proof of useful work" tokens are too speculative for institutional pitches. Real mainstream growth will come from stablecoins and tokenization, not AI tokens, within 2–3 years. He advises entering this market "with trepidation."

**Tokenized Stocks: Growth from the Edges**

Public companies are opting out of tokenized stock offerings at low rates because boards see no upside and downside risk. Hogan is unsurprised—committees say no by design. Long term, he does not think it matters. The misconception is that tokenized stocks will replace Schwab accounts; they won't. Instead, adoption will come from those underserved by traditional markets: international retail lacking US stock access and hedge funds needing 24/7/365 trading. His father (typical retail US investor) is the last person to adopt. The Avalanche/NISEY deal (24/7 tokenized stock venue launching later 2026) is a major win for Avalanche, lending credibility without requiring major corporate participation yet. Regulatory clarity from the SEC (equal rights, dividends, simple vehicles) is in place; adoption now hinges on time and pioneer wins proving the value.

**Institutional Tokenization Vision and Trade XYZ**

The vast majority of wealth managers (99%) cannot distinguish between Hyperliquid and Trade XYZ. However, the "1%" at cutting-edge wealth platforms see Trade XYZ and Hyperliquid's move into the US as "one of the most exciting things happening in the world." Institutions are excited about the Hyperliquid deal because it signals real-world adoption and participation in crypto infrastructure. They understand US market primacy and see tokenization/crypto as the future of finance.

**Hyperliquid as the Amazon of Finance**

Hogan draws an Amazon analogy: Amazon started as a bookstore and became an everything store by adding efficiency and multiple product lines to a single platform. Hyperliquid mirrors this—starting with crypto perpetuals, expanding to real-world assets and prediction markets, and now opening as an open-source platform. He sees it as building "a financial juggernaut" with the ambition to become one of the world's largest financial entities. Multiple growth rates stacked atop one another is the Bitwise investment thesis, and Hyperliquid exemplifies it.

**Fragmentation Risk: US vs. Rest-of-World Finance Internet**

Hogan worries about a bifurcated crypto world (analogous to China's internet vs. the global internet) if Democrats restrict DeFi harshly. However, he remains optimistic long term. Breakthroughs in automated compliance and privacy-preserving identity will allow much of global DeFi to operate frictionlessly within US regulatory frameworks, with only a small corner remaining true "piracy." The bulk of future finance, even globally, could be "more open than you're imagining" because automation and privacy layers will reduce friction.

**Bitcoin's Path to $1.6M: Store-of-Value Market Dynamics**

Hogan's $1.6 million Bitcoin projection by 2036 rests on two variables. First, the store-of-value market (Bitcoin + gold) currently at $30 trillion is growing 13% annually (gold's 22-year track record since the 2004 ETF). Continued growth yields a $90 trillion market by 2036. Second, Bitcoin currently holds 5% of that market; capturing 25% would mean $1.6 million per coin. Every trend—large wealth-management adoption, rising central-bank interest, steady ETF flows, regulatory progress, JP Morgan's institutional nod—points to Bitcoin's market share expanding, not shrinking. Zcash's outperformance is consistent with Bitcoin being "dragged into the regulatory light," creating room for privacy assets (10–15% of the store-of-value market). This does not detract from Bitcoin institutional adoption.

**Bitcoin vs. Zcash and the Privacy Thesis**

Hogan's base case is to own both. Bitcoin will likely capture most initial institutional flows (institutions don't yet know or trust Zcash), but Zcash, being much smaller, needs fewer flows for larger percentage gains and higher volatility. Zcash sits between Bitcoin (institutional-friendly) and Monero (pure privacy), offering institutional compatibility with privacy optionality. He expects an "80-20 split" where institutional adoption drives most of the cycle, and privacy solutions (including Zcash) capture the remainder. He does not rule out "OG crypto graduating into privacy solutions" and selling Bitcoin to institutions to buy Zcash as a cycle rotation.

**Tokenomics: Buybacks Work, Inflation Cuts Don't**

Buybacks clearly outperform. They signal alignment between projects and token holders, which implies that tokenomics will improve over time. Inflation reductions, conversely, have historically underperformed. Strong assets typically don't need to reduce inflation to stakeholders. From an alpha-generation perspective, buybacks and burns have positive price attribution; inflation adjustments do not. The takeaway is that transparency and alignment matter more than mechanical supply tweaks.

**Digital Asset Trusts: Resilient, Not Dead**

Hogan used to believe DATs would die except for one per ecosystem. However, data show DATs trade as a levered version of momentum Bitcoin, not as distinct products. If momentum returns, DAT premiums could recover. His revised base case: most DATs will evolve beyond simple Bitcoin holding (like Solana DATs experimenting with operating businesses), and large DATs will persist in each ecosystem. Peak DAT mania may have passed, but they are not structurally dead.
