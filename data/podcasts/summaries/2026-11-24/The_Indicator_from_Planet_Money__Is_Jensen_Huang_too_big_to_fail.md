# The Indicator from Planet Money — "Is Jensen Huang too big to fail?"

**Host:** Ricky Mulvey. **Guest:** Waylon Wong (co-host).

## TL;DR

- **Jensen Huang resigned as NVIDIA CEO following three affair allegations** — one involving an alleged Chinese spy named Alice Huang at a company suspected of illegally exporting advanced AI chips to China, and two with NVIDIA employees (Kimberly Powell and an anonymous person).
- **The board unanimously rejected his return, then unanimously reinstated him within days** — President Trump reportedly called the board to push for Huang's reinstatement after expressing support on X and in media appearances.
- **NVIDIA stock surged ~30% ($1 trillion in market value) on Huang's return** — the rally extended to the broader market (S&P 500 up ~5%) and especially to smaller GPU rental companies (neoclouds), which rose 18% as a group.
- **Huang has been a vocal champion of selling advanced AI chips to China** — he pitched the Chinese market as worth "tens of billions of dollars every year" to investors; Trump proposed a 25% government cut of China chip sales in December 2025.
- **Internal reports describe Huang as abusive toward employees** — The New York Post reported incidents including telling a VP "I hope you get the flu and I hope it gets worse," and using creative insults to demean staff.
- **NVIDIA appears too economically important to allow Huang's permanent departure** — the company is central to US AI infrastructure, with applications from black-hole data processing (10 terabytes per minute) to self-driving vehicles and broader AI advancement.

---

**The Scandal Speedrun**

The crisis began with rumors of an affair between Huang and Alice Huang, a woman working at Megaspeed, a technology firm under investigation by the US Commerce Department. Investigators suspected Megaspeed was using NVIDIA as a front to illegally export advanced AI chips to China in violation of export controls. The New York Times reported that NVIDIA hardware was found still sealed in boxes at a Megaspeed data center in Malaysia. Huang preempted fuller coverage by issuing a public apology to his wife and pledging to cooperate with NVIDIA's investigation. However, President Trump was separately briefed on the relationship in late fall 2025, with advisors flagging concern about NVIDIA's CEO allegedly romantically involved with an alleged Chinese spy.

Two additional affairs were then disclosed: one with Kimberly Powell, a leader in NVIDIA's healthcare unit, and another with an anonymous NVIDIA employee. The timing compressed—the second round broke just three days after Huang's initial apology letter. Reports included reference to a hotel video of Huang and Powell being affectionate, though journalists did not view the video themselves. The board convened an emergency meeting, and Huang resigned.

**The Reversal and Trump's Intervention**

Jensen Huang and shareholders did not accept the outcome. A petition at standwithjensen.com garnered over 10,000 signatures from current NVIDIA employees and over 100,000 from shareholders. Tech executives including Shopify CEO and Tesla CEO Elon Musk advocated for his return. On the morning of NVIDIA's first earnings call without Huang as CEO, Huang posted on X acknowledging his serious mistakes and his cooperation with the board investigation, then stated he had "seen so much support" and was "honored to take that call" if the board deemed his return in shareholders' and employees' best interests.

The board initially held firm, with chair Harvey Jones stating on the earnings call that the board "unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO." Within days, President Trump told a PBS reporter that Huang would "hopefully be back soon as CEO." Trump then went on CNBC and disclosed that he had called NVIDIA's board to urge Huang's reinstatement. The board then announced it had unanimously voted to bring Huang back—the same board, the same unanimous vote, reversed in under a week. Trump owned at least $12 million worth of NVIDIA stock at the end of 2025, positioning him as a substantial shareholder alongside his presidential authority.

**Market Reaction and Systemic Importance**

NVIDIA stock rose approximately 30% on Huang's reinstatement, representing roughly $1 trillion in market capitalization—on par with the entire market value of Walmart, the largest private employer in the US. The broader stock market surged, with the S&P 500 rising close to 5% the day Huang returned as CEO. GPU rental companies (neoclouds) rose 18% as a group. These smaller data centers are viewed as riskier investments because they often rely heavily on debt to build infrastructure and many are unprofitable; however, NVIDIA has backstopped some of them. CoreWeave, a neocloud that ran into credit market trouble, was told by NVIDIA to proceed with chip purchases and data center construction, with NVIDIA committing to pay for unused computing capacity.

**Huang's Vision and the "Too Big to Fail" Question**

Throughout the scandal, Huang has positioned himself as the architect of an AI revolution. He has told investors that AI will drive high-quality job creation and transform sectors from self-driving vehicles to scientific computing. NVIDIA's chips are central to this narrative: the company's supercomputers process black-hole data at a rate of 10 terabytes per minute—a process that previously took nine hours. Huang's reinstatement despite documented abusive behavior toward employees, alleged ties to espionage concerns, and his vocal push to sell advanced chips to China suggests the market and political leadership view him as indispensable. The episode concludes that the drama underscores how much of America's economic future and the world's is "riding on the AI bet."
