# Heavyweight — "2026 Update: Skye"

**Host:** Jonathan Goldstein (Gimlet Media; creator and host of Heavyweight). **Guest:** Sky (subject of the original "Sky" episode; mother who was excluded by friends at age 11 and pursued closure decades later).

## TL;DR

- **Clark, age 11, pushes his mother to confront childhood bullies** — he questions why Sky never asked the four girls who toilet-papered their house and wrote "fuck you" on the garage door why they did it, urging her to seek answers and "figure this out."
- **The original incident happened at age 11 — the girls were exclusive, popular, and excluded Sky after she refused to participate in rebellious pranks** — Tessa later explains Sky was a "goody two shoes" who didn't want to toilet paper houses or drink alcohol, making her a target for the exclusion message written in large white paint letters.
- **Three of four girls refused or evaded contact; only Tessa agreed to meet** — Sam claimed they "naturally grew apart," Nicky denied being there, Randy said the repeated messages felt creepy, but Tessa, the only one Sky actually met, acknowledged she was a "follower" who didn't want to be alone and stayed with the group out of peer pressure.
- **Sky's core conflict is timidity — she apologizes excessively, doesn't assert boundaries, and had avoided confrontation for 35 years** — Clark explicitly tells her she's "too timid" and wants her to focus on herself rather than accommodating others' feelings.
- **Tessa validated that Sky had done nothing wrong** — after meeting in California, Tessa told Sky she hadn't caused the incident, wished Sky hadn't felt guilty for 35 years, and the two embraced warmly.
- **Seven years later: Clark is now 18, a college freshman studying political science, and still angry at the three girls who didn't show up** — he felt fondness for Tessa but holds the other three accountable for refusing to take responsibility.
- **Sky's perspective shifted from seeking revenge to seeking understanding, and she modeled kindness without timidity** — she learned to listen to Tessa's perspective (that peer pressure, not malice, drove the behavior) while also standing up for herself.

**The Bedtime Ritual and the Untold Story**

Jonathan introduces the episode by explaining that Sky would tell Clark a particular story during their nightly bedtime ritual. When Clark reaches age 11—the same age Sky was when the incident happened—he begins questioning the moral of the story in ways he hadn't before. Sky had framed the events as teaching that "awful things do happen, but in the end everything turns out fine," but Clark recognizes that this doesn't account for the unanswered questions: Why did the girls do it? Why did Sky never ask? And why hasn't she asked even now, as an adult?

**The Original Incident: Exclusion and Anger**

At age 11, Sky was part of a tight-knit group of four girls who wore Esprit sweatshirts and watched *The Love Boat* on weekends—"the popular girls." One summer, after Sky's family moved away briefly, the girls toilet-papered Sky's house and painted "fuck you" in large white letters across the family's brown double garage door. The words were split across the two doors: "fuck" on one, "you" on the other. Sky's mother Rachel witnessed the girls making their getaway. The incident felt intentional and malicious to Sky because they had to bring paint, not just toilet paper—there was forethought and anger behind it.

**Why Sky Was Excluded: The "Goody Two Shoes"**

Sky avoided the girls throughout junior high and never confronted them. Her mother Rachel believed the incident had caused Sky to "pull in her wings," making her more withdrawn and careful about others' feelings. Decades later, when Sky finally meets Tessa in California, Tessa explains that Sky had been excluded because she was a "goody two shoes" compared to the other three girls. Those girls were "a little mischievous" and "a little rebellious," wanting to do "something bad." Sky didn't want to toilet paper houses or drink alcohol. Tessa suggests that to the other girls, being cast out by someone they perceived as morally superior felt like an insult—hence the explicit fuck you painted on the garage.

**Sky's Timidity and Clark's Push for Assertiveness**

Clark uses the story to pressure his mother to be less timid. He observes that she's too accommodating, apologizes excessively, and avoids asserting her own needs. He points out that her New Year's resolution was to say "no" more often, and he witnesses her getting steamrolled at school functions and settling for the wrong meal at restaurants rather than bother a waiter. Clark explicitly tells her she's "timid little mommy" and wants her to stop prioritizing everyone else's feelings and start focusing on herself.

**The Search for the Girls**

Jonathan helps Sky contact the four girls. Sam, whom Sky runs into at a high school reunion, becomes evasive on Facebook, claiming they "naturally grew apart" and eventually telling Sky that her repeated attempts to contact her feel "overwhelming and stressed." Nicky denies being there at all, saying she and Sky weren't even close. Randy sends an email saying the repeated messages feel creepy and that she's creeped out by the contact. Sky, feeling guilty for reaching out, apologizes excessively to Randy. Clark observes that she's "over apologetic," and Jonathan points out that apologizing for having reached out gives the other person power.

**The Meeting with Tessa**

Only Tessa agrees to meet Sky. The two meet in a studio in California, where Jonathan and Clark observe from a control booth. Tessa initially claims the memory is "really really vague" and that they "literally just stumbled upon" Sky's home while toilet papering. When pushed by Clark through a microphone, Tessa reveals more: she was a "follower," not a ringleader. She stayed with the group because "you'll stick with your friends even though you see things that you don't like in them" and "you just don't want to be alone." Tessa emphasizes that she wishes Sky hadn't felt guilty for 35 years and doesn't think Sky did anything wrong. She tells Sky directly: "You freakin dodged a bullet with not being with those girls."

**The Role of the Next Generation**

Tessa also reveals that her own daughter, nearly 13, initially refused to let her participate, asking "What's in it for you?" and worried Tessa would "come out as a bad person." After the podcast aired, Tessa's daughter listened to the episode and changed her mind—she told Tessa she was "thankful and happy" her mother participated, and said "middle school sucks," recognizing her own struggles in the story.

**2026 Update: Clark as an Adult**

Seven years after the original episode, Jonathan checks in. Clark is now 18, a freshman in college studying political science. He felt genuine fondness for Tessa and her willingness to show up, but he remains angry at the three girls who refused to take accountability. Sky, however, has evolved: she felt warm feelings and fondness for Tessa after the conversation, and those feelings "eclipsed" her old painful memories. She also acknowledges that Clark may change his perspective as he gets older and potentially has children of his own. Sky notes that she's grateful for Clark's insistence that she confront the situation and grateful for the closure meeting with Tessa provided.

**The Lesson: Kindness Without Timidity**

The core of the episode's arc is Sky learning that one can be kind without being timid—that kindness can carry strength. Clark, watching his mother interact with Tessa with warmth and honesty, tells her afterward: "You weren't timid, Mommy, you were brave." Rather than seeking revenge, Sky pursued understanding. She listened to Tessa's perspective about peer pressure and fear of isolation, validated it, and found that compassion could coexist with self-advocacy. Clark's insistence that she confront the past was less about punishment and more about teaching her that she deserved answers and closure—a lesson she learned and embodied.
