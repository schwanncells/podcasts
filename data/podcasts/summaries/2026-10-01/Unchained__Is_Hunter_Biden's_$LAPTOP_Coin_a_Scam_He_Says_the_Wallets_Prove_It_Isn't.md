**Hunter Biden's Path to Cryptocurrency**

Hunter Biden's introduction to blockchain came through humanitarian work, not speculation. As chairman of the UN World Food Program board—the world's largest humanitarian organization serving 75 million people across 80 countries—he encountered a systemic problem: refugees and subsistence farmers in places like Kabul, Darfur, and former refugee camps had no access to global markets because international banking infrastructure excluded them. The UN program created digital wallets allowing refugees and farmers to transact at a micro level via cellphones, enabling participation in a global economy that traditional banking had locked them out of. This experience shaped Biden's belief in blockchain as a tool for financial inclusion rather than speculation.

Biden did not engage with crypto publicly until 2024, when he read "The Internet of Money" and began forming stronger ideological positions. In June 2026, he tweeted a response to a question about fiat currency being "unicorn fart dust"—responding that "fiat is a sham," central banks are corrupt, and "decentralized digital currency and the blockchain are the inevitable future." He grounded this view in historical fact: the US dollar has lost approximately 97% of its value since 1913, and government ability to print currency at will severs any real relationship between money and value. He acknowledged that central banks have prevented some recessions and depressions, but he pointed to 2008 and the subsequent trillion-dollar stimulus and sticky inflation as evidence that fiat currency embeds chronic instability.

Biden rejected the common framing that cryptocurrency enables crime by virtue of being digital. He emphasized that any technology—a hammer, a financial system, blockchain—can be used to build or destroy depending on whose hands control it. If one wants to find criminal behavior, he argued, start with the banking industry, which has historically excluded populations from the global economy and moved money on behalf of "really really bad people."

**The $LAPTOP Coin: Intentional Anti-Trump Design**

When Biden launched the $LAPTOP memecoin in 2026, he was reacting directly to Trump's tokenization strategy. Trump promised to be the "crypto king" and instead launched a maximum-extraction play, taking money directly from his "most devout and hardcore followers" into his own pockets. Biden saw an opportunity to demonstrate that a memecoin could operate under opposite principles.

$LAPTOP inverts Trump's token economics entirely. Trump's token allocated 80% to insiders; $LAPTOP allocates 50% to community (airdrops) and 30% to founders and core team, with the remaining 20% to the foundation for charitable purposes. Biden personally holds 300 million founder tokens—all locked and untouchable, with a six-month lock-up followed by an 18-month linear vesting schedule. He emphasized that not a single token has been released and none will be until the vesting period begins. The remaining founder and team allocations are similarly locked; Biden stated with pride that no insider has extracted a single dollar from the project.

The $LAPTOP token includes an innovation Biden called "really really cool"—a prediction market embedded in the token mechanics. The market makes 30 predictions across a two-year period through 2029, spanning political outcomes ("Will Democrats win the midterms?"), cryptocurrency milestones ("Bitcoin hits all-time high"), and lighter predictions ("Will the Philadelphia Eagles win their home opener?"). When a prediction comes true, the equivalent token allocation is burned (removed from circulation). When a prediction fails, that percentage goes to charity. This creates a 5% minimum charity allocation, but the mathematics suggest it could reach 12–15% of total supply over the vesting period. Critically, this vesting schedule prevents sudden sell pressure—the charity portion vests on the same schedule as Biden's own holdings, ensuring the token's lifecycle extends beyond the typical six-month pump-and-dump.

Biden's broader intention was to get Democrats to pay attention to cryptocurrency policy. He argued that the Democratic party's dismissal of crypto stems not from stupidity but from ignorance—they hear only about Trump's grift and take talking points from party leadership without educating themselves. "The vast majority of Democrats... don't know. They haven't read anything about cryptocurrency... It's not because they're stupid. It's just because they think there are big things going on." By launching a well-designed token, Biden hoped to shift the conversation from "Is crypto a scam?" to "Why does the Democratic party ignore a technology that 20–30% of Americans already use?"

**The Launch Disaster and Liquidity Failure**

What should have been a controlled launch became a cautionary tale about institutional failure. Biden coordinated with Coinbase for a day-one listing and worked with market makers to establish initial liquidity. The decentralized exchange (DEX) launch required careful capital deployment: Biden estimated that 25% of available token supply and substantial cash reserves were necessary to absorb the expected surge in trading volume without creating exponential volatility.

Instead, the market makers deployed catastrophically inadequate liquidity. They deployed only $5,000 and approximately 29,000 tokens to the DEX, when they should have deployed closer to $500,000 in cash and 5 million tokens. This microscopic liquidity pool meant that a $6 purchase in the opening seconds increased the token's market cap by 5%. The math compounded exponentially: within one minute, the token price spiraled from the planned $50 million fully diluted valuation (FDV) to $317 billion—pure fiction unconnected to any real capital inflow. Within minutes, it collapsed back down to the $70–80 million range, where it now trades at $0.07–$0.08.

Biden called this "an incredibly poor decision" or "negligence" on the part of the market maker. He noted that the firm has internally admitted the error but emphasized that responsibility ultimately falls on him—the founder. However, he also stressed that blockchain transparency proves there was no scam: every wallet is visible, every transaction is recorded, and the promised token allocations are verifiable on-chain. Accusations that he was running the same extraction scheme as Trump could be definitively refuted by examining the blockchain wallets.

**Defending Against Scam Accusations and Vesting Mechanics**

When critics with small platforms (like an account with 18 followers) accused Hunter of doing the same thing Trump did—creating a scam while claiming to troll Trump—Biden's response was blunt: look at the blockchain. Every wallet, every token commitment, every vesting schedule is transparent and immutable. He hadn't put a penny in his own pocket. If anyone doubted whether the token had scam mechanics, they could verify every claim against the public ledger.

Biden emphasized that the token's viability depends on his continued engagement. If he vanished after six months or extracted tokens early, the entire project would collapse. But his innovation—the prediction market—locks him in for at least two years. "It's not like, oh six months come, I'd like go like this and walk away. I'm in it because the token has a lifespan that's much longer than that." The prediction market creates a natural lifecycle for the community's attention and engagement, extending far beyond the typical memecoin rush.

He also acknowledged mistakes in rollout. In hindsight, he should have spent more time in conversation with the crypto community before launching, so they would understand his genuine engagement with the space and the rigor behind the token design. "I should have spent a lot more time talking about it before I did it so that they could know that I don't come to this like somebody met me at a dinner party and said hey can we slap your name on something and see what happens." Instead, he had spent significant time learning the community, the technology, and the language—exactly the opposite of celebrity-name slapping.

**Corruption in the Trump Administration and Crypto Hypocrisy**

Biden spent considerable time discussing what he views as systemic corruption within the Trump family's use of government office for financial gain. He positioned his own minimal income ($200,000 per year from selling art while his father was president) against what he characterizes as vastly larger corruption.

Jared Kushner, serving as special envoy to the Middle East with no electoral mandate, ethics oversight, or security clearance requirements, has received approximately $100 million annually in fees from Saudi Arabia while negotiating that country's relationship with the rest of the world on nuclear technology, Iran policy, and regional affairs. The irony, Biden noted, is that Saudi Arabia was the first country to receive US assistance in building nuclear technology on foreign soil—all while the US purports to fight Iran over its nuclear program. The Saudis were principally responsible for 9/11, yet they are paying Kushner $100 million per year for his influence and proximity to US foreign policy.

Don Trump Jr. launched a private equity venture fund immediately before inauguration. The underlying companies have grown from zero to over $3.4 billion in Department of Defense contracts in a single year. They also received a $630 million loan guarantee—the largest the Pentagon has ever given a private company. Eric Trump holds billions in real estate deals not with individuals but with governments: Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Vietnam, and others.

Most strikingly, Jared Kushner's net wealth has increased by $16.5 billion since 2025. Biden argued that this "Mount Everest of corruption" dwarfs any potential misuse of executive power on his own part. The mechanism is clear: "You have to pay a toll to get anything done in Washington DC through this administration—from foreign policy to pardons to anything."

Biden acknowledged that he cannot speak for the Biden administration's specific policy positions on crypto, but he noted that the media has so many scandals to cover (jets given by Qatar, the ballroom, Arlington National Cemetery burials, stock trades) that the broader pattern becomes invisible. In the first quarter of 2024 alone, Trump completed 3,400 stock trades—more than every other president combined has done in their entire presidencies.

**Democratic Party Blindness to Cryptocurrency**

Biden's central argument is that the Democratic party's rejection of crypto stems from willful ignorance, not strategic disagreement. They have no stake in understanding blockchain because their only exposure has been Trump's grift. The vast majority of Democratic leadership has not read about cryptocurrency, do not listen to crypto podcasts, and do not engage with the technology at all. Instead, they absorb talking points from committee leadership and dismiss the entire space.

This matters because 20–30% of Americans hold cryptocurrency in some form across Coinbase, Binance, Kraken, Robinhood, Revolut, and OKX. When Democratic leaders call crypto stupid, a grift, or worthless—saying "Bitcoin's going to zero," "the blockchain is [expletive]"—they are insulting one in every five voters. Biden found this extraordinarily shortsighted. "What are we talking about? It's like one out of every five people. How offensive is that when you say what they're doing is not just stupid... but it's a sham, it's a grift, it's all a big lie?"

He also emphasized the broader strategic value of blockchain in an age of AI and misinformation. As AI systems become more capable and generative media becomes harder to verify, immutable transparent ledgers become more important, not less. Blockchain offers a cryptographic proof of authenticity that the centralized internet increasingly cannot provide. This is true in the art world—where Biden registers every painting on the blockchain to verify authenticity in perpetuity—and true in the financial world, where transparent, verifiable transactions prevent the kind of hidden derivatives trading and off-books manipulation that led to 2008.

**Elizabeth Warren and Democratic Alignment with Banking**

Laura Shen raised a point that resonated deeply: Democrats have aligned with big banks on stablecoin regulation, in direct opposition to their 2008 messaging about financial system corruption. Biden responded with frustration. Elizabeth Warren—whom he said he respects as a person—is taking the banking industry's side. His argument was precise: Warren's primary concern about cryptocurrency is the risk of illicit use enabled by anonymity. But where was this concern applied to the 2008 crisis, when the entire banking industry robbed Americans of trillions of dollars and no one went to jail?

After 2008, the government printed trillions in new currency to bail out banks. The real cost fell on Americans through inflation—what Biden called "an inherent tax that continues forward and forward on American people." When new money is printed, the banks receive it first, before inflation hits, so they capture the gains. Ordinary Americans see the value of their money decrease. This is why "fiat is a sham"—not because it's a radical or controversial statement, but because the system is structurally designed to extract wealth from ordinary savers.

Meanwhile, blockchain technology could eliminate 15% transaction fees that remittance companies charge to send money to family in Ghana or Mexico City. It could let subsistence farmers bring grain to markets in Nairobi without intermediaries. It could free an entire globe from the banking system's gatekeeping and fees. Yet Warren, the senator who once challenged banking corruption, is now aligned with those same institutions against a technology that could disintermediate them. Biden found this incomprehensible.

**Blockchain as Tool for Art and Financial Inclusion**

Biden returned repeatedly to personal applications of blockchain that had shaped his worldview. As an artist, blockchain has been transformative. Every painting he creates is registered on-chain via Fair (a blockchain registry). This permanent, cryptographically verified record means he can forever prove authenticity and provenance, even after the work leaves his hands. In a world where deep fakes and generative AI are making verification harder, this kind of immutable record becomes more valuable, not less.

He also noted that he accepts cryptocurrency for paintings (though he joked that so far no one had paid him in Bitcoin). The ability to receive payment directly without intermediaries—without art dealers, payment processors, or tax authorities taking a cut—represents a kind of economic freedom that the traditional financial system doesn't offer.

Beyond art, he pointed back to his original inspiration: the UN World Food Program's digital wallet program. For refugees and subsistence farmers with only a cellphone, a digital wallet and blockchain-based transactions represent the difference between economic exclusion and global participation. They make possible what was previously impossible. This is the technology that Elizabeth Warren is siding with big banks against.

**Prospects for Crypto Regulation and the Clarity Act**

Shen asked about the Clarity Act—comprehensive crypto legislation that died in part due to political disputes over ethics laws. Biden acknowledged that passing such legislation in the next two years would be "very very difficult" even if Democrats take the House or Senate. The legislative calendar is crowded, and momentum on crypto issues is limited.

However, he emphasized that crypto advocates cannot wait for politicians to understand the issue on their own. Advocates like Shen, like himself, and like independent voters must educate their constituents and elected representatives directly. Warren's resistance to crypto is not rooted in her understanding of blockchain—it's rooted in her alignment with the financial institutions she once opposed. The path forward is constituent pressure, not hope that Congress will act unprompted.

Biden expressed confidence that eventually the technology will prevail because it solves real problems. The question is whether Democratic leadership will be on the wrong side of history, fighting against 20–30% of the electorate's financial preferences and a technology that enables global financial inclusion.
