# The Indicator from Planet Money — "Community colleges are kind of underrated"

**Host:** Robert Smith, Adrian Ma. **Guest:** Eric Garcia Luna (economist, Minneapolis Federal Reserve Bank).

## TL;DR

- **Community colleges growing faster than four-year colleges nationwide** — enrollment at community colleges is expanding at a higher rate despite being undervalued in perception.
- **Older adults returning to community college at increasing rates** — the Minneapolis Fed reported a 44% increase in adult students over three years, primarily in health science programs like nursing and dental hygiene.
- **Nursing degrees affordable and attainable at community colleges** — William Leitzman earned a nursing credential for $12,000 total and can become a registered nurse after passing a licensing exam, making it significantly cheaper than four-year college alternatives.
- **Healthcare sector driving enrollment growth** — regional investment in healthcare infrastructure and worker shortages in nursing and related fields are attracting older workers seeking career transitions or better-paying professions.
- **AI and automation concerns motivating career switches** — workers are pursuing community college credentials partly due to concerns about job security in the face of AI and tariffs, seeking automation-resistant careers.
- **Community college reputation improving as four-year costs soar** — the cost and time commitment of traditional four-year degrees are making community college an increasingly attractive option for career changers, especially those with mortgages or family responsibilities.

**The Beige Book and Economic Signals**

Robert Smith and Adrian Ma open the episode by celebrating the 56th anniversary of the Beige Book, the Federal Reserve's semiannual collection of economic anecdotes from the 12 regional Federal Reserve banks. The "Beigy Awards" format allows them to highlight the best economic stories from the most recent Beige Book. The Beige Book serves as a window into localized economic conditions and workforce trends that national statistics often miss.

**Construction Industry Weakness**

Two runner-up awards go to stories about the construction industry. The Atlanta Fed reported that builders are experiencing sharp traffic declines and growing buyer hesitancy, with one builder noting that even at lower price points they cannot move inventory. The Cleveland Fed contributed a note that construction workers are reporting being nervous and stressed about cooling demand for their skills. High interest rates have slowed new home sales in parts of the country, creating uncertainty in an industry that depends on cyclical demand.

**The Winning Story: Community College Enrollment Surge**

The Minneapolis Fed wins the award with a story about older adults returning to community college to obtain second credentials and better compete in the job market. This observation prompted further investigation into whether community colleges are experiencing genuine growth. The team discovered that community colleges are indeed growing faster nationwide than four-year colleges, and they traveled to Normandale Community College south of Minneapolis to investigate further.

**Normandale's Adult Student Growth**

Tim LaPam, the new student enrollment coordinator at Normandale Community College, confirmed significant growth in adult student enrollment. The college has seen a 44% increase in adult students over the past three years, with much of that growth concentrated in health science cohorts including nursing, dental hygiene, and Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA) programs. LaPam notes that some students are motivated by feeling underemployed and seeking better-paying professions, while others are leaving jobs that were not fulfilling and searching for more meaningful career paths.

**William Leitzman's Career Transition**

William Leitzman, a 39-year-old who taught English in South Korea for 15 years, exemplifies the trend. After researching career options, he discovered that nursing is one of the top fields and decided it aligned with both logic and his heart. He learned that he could earn an associate degree in nursing and become a registered nurse for approximately $12,000 total, a stark contrast to the cost and time commitment of four-year programs. While nursing school is "very, very difficult," Leitzman adapted to being an older student in a class where most students are under 25. His classmates were welcoming and supportive.

**Why Workers Are Turning to Community College**

When asked why more older workers are pursuing community college credentials, Leitzman cites several factors. He mentions AI as a concern driving some workers to seek automation-proof jobs. Beyond that, he observes that community college is developing a better reputation, and it is becoming increasingly difficult to justify the expense of a four-year college degree. For workers with mortgages, family responsibilities, or a need for quick career pivots, spending tens of thousands of dollars and years in a traditional four-year program is often not rational. Leitzman adds that nursing is becoming "really hot right now" and describes it as "kind of like the new tech degree."
