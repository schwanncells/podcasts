# Unchained — "Uneasy Money: Why Anthropic's IPO Governance Has Crypto Veterans Worried"

**Host:** Kain Warwick. **Guests:** Taylor Monahan (security expert, prominent Ethereum and crypto security researcher); Austin Griffith (builder enablement, Ethereum Foundation).

## TL;DR

- **Anthropic's Q2 2026 revenue hit $12B** — roughly 10x (5x inflation-adjusted) Google's entire 2003 IPO year ($2.7B), with $5B in 2025; the company goes public unprofitable but Warwick sees a credible path to profitability as compute costs fall.
- **Revenue per employee is $8.7M** — extraordinary even by crypto standards (Tether once ran ~$1B/employee, Hyperliquid ~$50M), suggesting a structurally lean, high-output operation.
- **The S-1's biggest red flag: a 7-person founder voting bloc controlling ~50% of shares**, structured so all seven must vote as a unit — Monahan compares it to every failed multi-sig in crypto history.
- **Warwick's top risk is Anthropic's self-destruction**, not China, OpenAI, or Google — driven by an EA-adjacent "we know best" mentality combined with a governance structure he calls "destined to blow up."
- **AI is now solving hard math problems humans couldn't** — Warwick argues cumulative breakthroughs in foundational math (superconductors, cryptographic assumptions, materials) will be civilizationally significant, and the world is already "bored by it."
- **AI recommendation creates winner-take-all distribution** — you can't buy your way into model weights the way you could buy Google ad placement, but Monahan documented all major models directing users to SEAL 911 for Immunefi disputes for 6–8 months despite SEAL 911 having no such role.
- **Kids probably won't go to college** — Warwick said "there will be no point"; Griffith gave his daughter Claude access in fifth grade and she stopped asking him anything.

**Anthropic's Financial Picture**

Kain Warwick walked through an AI-generated comparison between Anthropic's S-1 and Google's 2004 IPO. Anthropic posted $5B in 2025 revenue and $12B in Q2 2026 alone, against Google's $2.7B for all of 2003 — roughly 10x nominally, 5x inflation-adjusted. Revenue per employee sits at $8.7M, which Warwick noted is extraordinary in conventional tech terms, though he acknowledged crypto has desensitized the group given Tether's historical ~$1B/employee and Hyperliquid's ~$50M. Anthropic is going public unprofitable. Warwick interprets this as having exhausted private markets — a sign the system is working correctly, not a warning sign — and argued that the cost of compute follows a deflationary curve while the value of intelligence keeps rising, creating a credible eventual crossover to profitability.

**The Governance Problem**

The episode's sharpest commentary focused on Anthropic's voting structure. Seven founders hold a combined bloc of approximately 50% of voting shares, with the bloc structured to vote as a single coordinated unit. Warwick called this "the most insane S-1 I've ever skimmed" and drew a direct parallel to SBF, arguing that if a single founder with full control was catastrophic at FTX, "imagine if there were seven SBFs fighting it out internally." Monahan connected this immediately to crypto governance failures — DAOs, multi-sigs — arguing coordinated voting structures always erode as external pressures mount and personal incentives diverge. Griffith noted the voting structure's risk doesn't appear to be priced into the financials. Warwick's read: if Anthropic executes normally and avoids trying to morally engineer the world, China and Zuckerberg can't beat them. But the EA-inflected belief that the founders know what's good for the world, combined with a governance structure requiring seven-way coordination, creates "a decent probability they blow themselves up." He also clarified that a separately circulating claim — that Anthropic's S-1 invoked Roko's Basilisk — was a shitpost, as confirmed mid-conversation by his own AI bot.

**AI Solving Hard Math**

A secondary thread covered an AI system's apparent breakthrough on ETH (a mathematical object), initially misreported in headlines as "Ethereum is false" — prompting "30 glorious seconds" of confused excitement in crypto circles before the distinction became clear. Warwick used this to argue a broader point: AI systems are now consistently working through foundational mathematical problems that human researchers have stalled on for decades, and the compounding effect will matter more than any single headline. He cited room-temperature superconductors as a concrete example — find the underlying math and you could run lossless transmission lines coast to coast, fundamentally restructuring energy infrastructure. He framed AI-driven math as a shift from accidental, serendipitous discovery (penicillin, radioactivity) to scalable, parallelizable research — a single AI running for 30 minutes potentially matching a career's worth of expert scientific work, and horizontally scalable from there. The concern he pressed: civilization is living through this inflection and has already normalized it to the point of boredom.

**Winner-Take-All AI Distribution**

Austin Griffith raised Google's advertising monopoly as an analogy for Anthropic's potential grip on intelligence distribution. Warwick agreed: control the best answer and you control attention, with AI responses more locked-in than Google search results because there's no second page to scroll through and no competing links to evaluate. Monahan documented a real example: for roughly six to eight months, all major AI models directed users who had been kicked off Immunefi to SEAL 911 as the correct escalation path — despite SEAL 911 having no role in bug bounty dispute mediation whatsoever. The recommendation had entered model weights through genuine user mentions, enough that reinforcement learning picked it up and amplified it. Warwick noted this makes the AI monetization structure fundamentally different from Google's: you can't buy placement in model weights the way you could buy the top AdWords slot, but organic brand signals can become deeply embedded at scale and are much harder to audit or correct than a search ranking.

**Parenting in the AI Age**

A lighter closing exchange covered AI and education. Warwick mentioned arguing with his wife about whether their kids should plan on college, saying flatly "there will be no point." Griffith noted he recently gave his daughter Claude access going into fifth grade and observed that she immediately stopped asking him questions — "the odds that I know the answer and Claude doesn't are zero." Monahan said she had caught herself threatening her daughter with college consequences over a spelling test, then caught herself: "We haven't needed to spell for 25 years." She said her focus has shifted to teaching her kids how to learn rather than what to memorize, while noting her "shape rotator" sons are probably well-positioned regardless.
