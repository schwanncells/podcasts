# This American Life — "884: The Idiot"

**Host:** Ira Glass. **Guest:** M. Gessen (journalist; author; staff writer at The New Yorker; host of the five-part Serial/New York Times podcast "The Idiot").

## TL;DR

- **Allen Gessen hired an FBI undercover agent to murder his ex-wife Priscilla** — after being introduced through a money-laundering investigation, Allen agreed in under a second to the "more permanent" option over deportation, saying "I would like to proceed" and "very happy to proceed with it."
- **Allen had already researched murder-for-hire before the FBI meeting** — he told the undercover agent he'd gotten quotes from Israelis, Eastern Europeans, and Italians, with the lowest estimate coming in at $220,000; the FBI's offer was roughly half that.
- **Allen twice abducted his son O from the boy's mother** — first taking him from Russia to the US when O was five, then attempting a flight from Montreal to London (arrested at the airport) when O was around eight; O spent two and a half years separated from his mother.
- **Priscilla endured a documented campaign of harassment while trying to get her son back** — stranded in Moscow without Russian language skills, she was later beaten by thugs in Zimbabwe and imprisoned twice there; she believes Allen was behind it.
- **O's first reunion with his mother was at a Concord, MA tea house after two-plus years apart** — he was shaking when she arrived; he asked her for "blue porridge," a baby food she had carried with her from Zimbabwe, and she made it for him on the spot.
- **Allen was convicted of murder-for-hire and sentenced to the 10-year maximum** — the jury rejected his defense that he only wanted Priscilla deported; at sentencing his lawyer repeated that claim, and the judge responded that what he described was kidnapping, punishable by up to 20 years.
- **The episode is a preview/frame for Gessen's five-part Serial podcast of the same name** — all episodes were released the same week; Gessen describes having spent 35 hours talking to Allen in prison and developing genuine compassion for him, with a theory of the case distinct from both Allen's and the prosecution's.

**The Family and the First Abduction**

M. Gessen introduces Allen as a first cousin who arrived in the US from Moscow in 1990 at age fifteen. She describes him as "a clown, a blowhard, a pompous ass" who called himself an entrepreneur and lived in Russia, Ukraine, and Zimbabwe running increasingly shady ventures including diamond mining and security work for Israeli mining companies. In 2019, Allen sent a Facebook message to the American side of the family announcing he had arrived in the US with his five-year-old son O, warning that his estranged wife Priscilla "might make attempts to contact you with requests detrimental to mine and O's interests." Gessen immediately texted her brother: "So our cousin has kidnapped his son and abandoned his daughter?"

Allen's father's sister Lenna had come with them. Both offered justifications — Allen claimed Priscilla was a bad mother who partied and used drugs; Lenna claimed she didn't read to O and kept only one book in the house. Priscilla, meanwhile, was stranded in Russia. She had a baby daughter whose birth certificate she couldn't obtain, which meant neither of them could leave the country. She texted Gessen's father begging for help finding O; he ignored the message.

**Gessen as Double Agent**

Without telling any of the men in her family, Gessen reached out to Priscilla and began quietly tracking Allen and Lenna's movements. She obtained their New York City address by offering to explain the school district system and asking for the address under that pretense. When they moved to Massachusetts, she found that address too. She relayed Priscilla's contact information to a Justice Department specialist in international child abduction cases and helped Priscilla initiate a Hague Convention petition.

Over the same period, Allen and Lenna built a conspicuously attentive life for O in Concord — Jewish school, violin, fencing, horseback riding, brogues and fedora hats. Gessen describes O as "a delight, curious, entertaining without being overbearing, and unfailingly polite," and admits she found Allen to be, against her expectations, a kind and devoted father.

**The Second Abduction and Arrest**

After Priscilla finally reached the US and the Hague case was set to be heard in federal court in Boston, she and O were briefly reunited. About four months later, Allen was arrested in Montreal at the airport while attempting to board a flight to London with O and Lenna, without Priscilla's knowledge. He was held in Canadian detention, then in a jail in upstate New York, and released on his own recognizance. O went into foster care for two days before Priscilla could reach him.

Gessen contrasts how she had experienced Allen's second kidnapping — as an absurd story she told at dinner parties, quoting Lenna's dramatic Facebook dispatches and a video of Allen singing a Russian prison song in a striped uniform — with how Priscilla and O experienced it: O watching his father tackled and handcuffed by armed agents, then being taken away by strangers.

**The FBI Meeting at The Boca Raton**

Allen was introduced to an FBI undercover agent (code name David, badge UC 4735) through a money-laundering investigation of his business partner Alex Kisolov. Allen believed he was meeting David to arrange a bribe of a government official to get Priscilla deported. The meeting took place June 2, 2022 at the Boca Raton Resort in Boca Raton, Florida.

On the recordings played at trial, Allen tells David that Priscilla is "a woman who will go the length of the world to make my life miserable" and that getting her deported is "the first order of business." He prices the deportation scheme — $100,000 — as "eminently reasonable," adding that he'd pay more in child support if it didn't happen. Then Allen says offhandedly, "Incidentally, if there's a cheaper way to get rid of her, I'm happy to do it, of course." David opens the door: "there is a cheaper way and probably a more permanent way to do it." Allen replies: "She's dead." David: "Yeah. I mean, that's up to you." Allen: "Uh, I would like to proceed."

Gessen notes that the time between David saying "that's up to you" and Allen's agreement is a fraction of a second — no breath, no pause, no request for clarification, no question about the price. Allen then disclosed that he had already researched this option: he'd gotten quotes from Israelis, Eastern Europeans, and Italians, with the lowest coming in at $220,000. David's associates, he suggested, could do it for roughly half that. Allen confirmed repeatedly that he understood what he was agreeing to — "more definite" was his word, meaning permanent, meaning dead. His only stated concern: that the children not be present to witness "violence."

Gessen, who had attended previous trials with skepticism about undercover techniques, says this was different: "I couldn't imagine any alternative interpretation of the tape I'd just heard."

**The Trial and Sentencing**

Allen took the stand in his own defense — unusual in criminal trials — and argued that every exchange on tape and in text was a vocabulary misunderstanding, that he only wanted Priscilla deported. The jury convicted him quickly. At sentencing, his lawyer made the same deportation argument, and the judge cut it off: the crime Allen's lawyer was describing was kidnapping, she said, punishable by up to 20 years. She sentenced Allen to the maximum for murder-for-hire: 10 years.

**The Broader Reporting and "The Idiot" Podcast**

Gessen describes the episode as a prologue to a five-part Serial/New York Times podcast of the same name, all episodes of which were released the same week. In her reporting, she interviewed Priscilla at length, learning the full scope of what Priscilla had endured between the two kidnappings — the months stranded in Moscow, the beatings and imprisonment in Zimbabwe she attributes to Allen, the two-and-a-half-year separation from O.

After Allen was incarcerated, Gessen spent 35 hours talking to him. She says he started by trying to sell her the same story the jury rejected, but over time the conversations shifted. "I genuinely felt compassion for him," she tells Glass. She developed her own theory of Allen's actions — distinct from Allen's account and from the prosecution's — which she leaves for listeners of the full podcast to discover. The show's title, she explains, is a reference to Dostoyevsky's novel: "I think I'm giving him the grace of perceiving what he did as just an incredibly dumb thing, and not only a very scary, mean, and evil thing."

Glass closes by noting that Serial Productions' first season, the Adnan Syed case in 2014, drew 20 million downloads per episode and essentially launched the true crime podcast genre. He describes "The Idiot" as returning Serial to true crime roots, with Gessen's personal stake in the story adding something he hadn't heard before.
