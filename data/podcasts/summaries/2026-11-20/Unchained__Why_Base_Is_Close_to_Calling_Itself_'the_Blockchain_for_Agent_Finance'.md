# Unchained — "Why Base Is Close to Calling Itself 'the Blockchain for Agent Finance'"

**Host:** Laura Shin. **Guest:** Jesse Pollak (head of Base, Coinbase's Ethereum layer-2 network; handed the Base app to Coinbase's Kobe about four months before the recording, now focused solely on the chain).

## TL;DR

- **Base metrics are growing week over week** — Pollak says trading, payments and financing are all accelerating, driven by a technical upgrade, new products like tokenized equities, and a sharper go-to-market.
- **Tokenized equities crossed $1 billion in weekly volume** — Base is now the second-largest on-chain financing market after Ethereum (passing Solana), with about $30 million of tokenized equities borrowed four weeks after that product launched.
- **Block times dropped from two seconds to 200 milliseconds** — a 10x improvement aimed at market makers, searchers and high-frequency traders; Base has done four upgrades since moving to its own stack (starting with "Azul"), plans a fifth this year and hopes for roughly 20 next year.
- **B20 is an ERC-20 equivalent for real-world assets** — it bakes in compliance, privacy and ISO memo controls, is fully permissionless, and has about 250 assets listed with billions in trading volume.
- **Product-first, not partnership-first** — Pollak contrasts Base with rivals making headline deals (ICE, Hyperliquid, DTCC), pointing to the Broadridge voting/dividend standard and Citi accepting USDC into business accounts on Base.
- **Agents are the thesis** — Pollak expects agents to be "the majority of actors in the new global economy," and says Base is close to rebranding from "blockchain for global finance" to "blockchain for agent finance"; agents already use x402 for one- or two-cent micropayments for inference and data.
- **A Base token remains open** — Base is "not in a rush" and "no additional comment," only that a token could be "a potentially powerful lever" for decentralization.

---

**Growth and the Product Focus**

Pollak says Base's core metrics are growing week over week across trading, payments and financing. He credits three things: a technical upgrade, products that did not exist four months earlier (notably tokenized equities, which crossed $1 billion of weekly volume), and a clearer go-to-market. On trading, he cites demand for spot crypto majors, tokenized equities and increasingly FX between dollar and non-dollar stablecoins. Base remains the preferred USDC rail, with billions of dollars of payments volume per month, an increasing share driven by agents. On financing, he says Base recently passed Solana to become the second-biggest on-chain financing market after Ethereum.

**Positioning Against Other Chains and TradFi Deals**

Shin asks how Base fits in a market where Vitalik has de-emphasized the L2 scaling strategy and rivals are announcing deals with ICE, Hyperliquid, LayerZero and DTCC. Pollak answers that Base's mission is to be "the blockchain for global finance," and that he cares most about products people actually use rather than "pre-announced name brand flashy partnerships." He argues that Base's four years in market, billions of dollars of live assets, deep financing markets and high yields make it the default when businesses decide where to build. He cites the Broadridge collaboration, which defines standardized on-chain voting and dividend handling for tokenized equities, and a Coinbase-enabled change letting Citi accept USDC directly into business bank accounts.

**Technical Upgrades: 200ms Blocks and B20**

Pollak says the pace of upgrades began when Base took control of its own technology stack at the start of the year rather than relying on a separate open-source stack. Since the first upgrade (Azul, around February or March) there have been four, with a fifth planned before year end. The latest cut block times from two seconds to 200 milliseconds, which he says is "faster than a human can perceive." B20 is a token standard oriented toward real-world assets, giving issuers of stablecoins, tokenized equities or private funds compliance, privacy and memo primitives out of the box. It is not a tokenization platform and does not compete with issuers' platforms. The current version is permissionless, works across DeFi, and lists about 250 assets.

**Tokenized Equities and the US Market**

The first tokenized equity product launched outside the US, just before the SEC's innovation exemption, because Base wanted a fully backed, one-to-one product with no debt structure and with voting rights and dividends. Pollak praises the SEC and CFTC as engaging "in good faith," saying the change is "night and day," and says Base wants to bring the product to the US in the coming months. Asked about issuers' reactions, including the OKX/ICE dispute, he says it is early, that education is required, and that Coinbase's approach is "measured, slow and steady" rather than picking public fights with company CEOs.

**Base and the Coinbase Wallet**

Shin notes that the Base app has been renamed Coinbase Wallet and is adding non-Base chains. Pollak says he trusts Kobe, a trader who is better placed to build trading products, and that his own job is making Base the best infrastructure so that every wallet, broker and neobank gravitates to it. He mentions a pump.fun collaboration giving rewards to people trading assets on Base, and claims the most liquid Bitcoin, Ethereum and equities spot markets.

**The Base Token**

On the token Base said it would explore more than a year ago, Pollak says the original decision to skip a token was meant to avoid distraction and to keep product-market fit readings clean. Decentralization and ecosystem alignment make a token a possible lever, but Base is "continuing to explore, but also not being in a rush," and he declines further comment.

**The Agentic Future**

Pollak says Base is close to describing itself as "the blockchain for agent finance." Growth today comes from agentic payments (x402 micropayments for inference and trading data) and agentic finance (agents running trading strategies and market-making). He cites a thesis, echoed in a recent tweet by "Brian", that teams of agents built on open models could outpace a single frontier model, "in the same way that a team of people can often outpace the smartest individual." He expects an open on-chain marketplace of agents to emerge because "agents naturally gravitate towards programmable money because they are programmable themselves," and says Coinbase will say much more about it in 2027.
