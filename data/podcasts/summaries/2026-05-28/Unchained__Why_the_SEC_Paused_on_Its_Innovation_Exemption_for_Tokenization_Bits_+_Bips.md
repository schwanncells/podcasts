# Unchained — "Why the SEC Paused on Its Innovation Exemption for Tokenization: Bits + Bips"

**Hosts:** Austin Campbell (founder, Zero Knowledge Group); Chris Perkins (CEO, 250 Digital Asset Management). **Guest:** Arjun Sati (President, Payword — the rebranded holding entity encompassing Kraken, Ninja Trader, XOX, Reap, Bitnomial, and related acquisitions).

## TL;DR

- **Payword spent $2.75 billion on M&A over 12 months** — acquisitions include Bitnomial (the only crypto-native CFTC trifecta: FCM, DCM, DCO), Reap (stablecoin payment and card issuance), Ninja Trader (active trader distribution), Small Exchange (derivatives), and XOX via Backed (tokenized equities, ~$25 billion in transaction volume in its first eight months).
- **Reap tripled revenue in 2025, almost entirely from emerging markets** — the thesis is that businesses in Lagos, Buenos Aires, Manila, and Karachi have "maybe three" card issuance choices globally; Reap fills the gap, profitably from day one.
- **SEC paused its innovation exemption for tokenized equities after pushback from Citadel, SIFMA, and JP Morgan** — two sticking points: a draft provision allowing synthetic tokens without issuer consent, and unresolved obligations under the Investment Company Act of 1940 and the Bank Secrecy Act (banks cannot pay dividends to anonymous on-chain addresses due to sanctions rules).
- **Commissioner Hester Pierce drew a hard line at issuer-led, issuer-consent tokens only** — Sati called the pause a "corporate plumbing problem," not a tokenization defeat; his watch signal is the first major American company choosing to issue natively on chain.
- **Incumbent resistance is about protecting payment for order flow economics** — DeFi's 24/7 permissionless trading bypasses NBBO (National Best Bid and Offer) routing and the toll-booth market-maker economics that generate billions annually for Citadel and others; Perkins recalled a Wall Street veteran saying "don't mess with my equities or we're going to have a problem."
- **DeFi TVL is down 50% since October** — Perkins raises this as an honest counterpoint to tokenization optimism; institutional rails (DTCC Russell 1000 pilot, Black Rock, Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, Circle, Ripple) are being laid on both sides regardless.
- **Pope Leo XIV's encyclical named cryptocurrency explicitly** — hosts see the Catholic subsidiarity principle (decisions at the lowest competent level) as philosophically aligned with crypto's anti-concentration thesis; Sati traces a line from Augustine's "City of God" through the Bitcoin white paper.
- **Payword's biggest self-identified risk: hidden leverage** — Sati cites near-zero loss ratios over 15 years using fully collateralized programs and unified risk engines, and frames crypto's transparency as an opportunity to avoid the opacity endemic to traditional finance.

**Payword's $2.75 Billion M&A Stack**

Arjun Sati reframes Kraken/Payword not as "just a bigger crypto exchange" but as a foundational layer for a global capital market: 24/7 spot, derivatives, payments, custody, tokenized equities, and Fed rail settlement under a unified jurisdictional licensing stack. Each acquisition fills a specific role. Bitnomial contributes the only crypto-native CFTC trifecta (exchange, clearing house, brokerage), absorbing more than a decade of regulatory work in a single transaction. Reap adds stablecoin payment and card issuance for emerging markets, profitable since day one and tripling revenue in 2025. Ninja Trader provides active retail trader distribution. Small Exchange covers small-account derivatives. XOX handles tokenized equities and recorded roughly $25 billion in transaction volume over its first eight months — Sati's characterization: "we don't think that's success, we just think that's the beginning."

The rebranding to Payword signals what's coming: Payword Asset Management and Payword Banking as forthcoming products, with banking licenses pursued in markets outside the US where regulators permit it.

**Emerging Markets as the Real Growth Engine**

Sati repeatedly returns to the pattern visible in Reap's numbers: the addressable market for financial infrastructure in Africa, Latin America, and Southeast Asia dwarfs what is visible from a US-centric perspective. A small-to-medium business in Lagos or Manila wanting card issuance may have three global choices; Reap is one of them. The same access dynamic drives XOX tokenized equity demand — users in emerging markets want to hold, invest in, earn yield on, and borrow against US equities the same way American investors take those capabilities for granted. Hyperliquid already has $2.5 billion in open interest; Trade.xyz has done $22 billion in volume, with the top underlying assets being Tesla, Nvidia, Apple, and Amazon — driven by global name recognition, not US retail.

**The SEC Innovation Exemption Pause**

Bloomberg's Scott Patterson reported the SEC was moving forward with an innovation exemption for tokenized equities; a day or two later, the agency paused. Campbell traced the sticking points to two provisions: (1) a draft that would have permitted third-party synthetic token wrappers without the underlying company's consent; and (2) unresolved obligations under the Investment Company Act of 1940 and the Bank Secrecy Act — most concretely, how to pay dividends to anonymous on-chain addresses when sanctions rules prohibit payments to unidentified persons.

Commissioner Hester Pierce issued a statement clarifying the exemption was always intended to cover "digital representations of the same underlying equity security," not synthetics. Sati read the pause as a "corporate plumbing problem" on specific provisions rather than a signal against tokenization. His suggested leading indicator: the first major American company choosing to issue stock natively on chain. Background rails are already being built — DTCC is running a Russell 1000 pilot, and Black Rock, Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, Circle, and Ripple are active regardless of the exemption's fate.

**Incumbent Resistance and Market Structure**

Perkins walked through why Citadel, SIFMA, and JP Morgan pushed back hardest in their January meetings with the SEC's crypto task force. The current US equity market is built around Reg NMS and the NBBO — rules requiring all executions to route to the best published price. Payment for order flow captures value along that routing. DeFi trading on permissionless 24/7 rails eliminates NBBO calculation entirely, bypassing the market-maker economics that generate billions annually for incumbent firms. Perkins recalled a Wall Street veteran telling him: "Do whatever you want with your silly little tokens, but don't mess with my equities or we're going to have a problem."

Both hosts were careful to note that not all incumbent opposition is purely self-interested — questions about shadow shorting, systemic risk, and whether DeFi infrastructure is ready for institutional volume are legitimate. But the framing of economic self-interest as regulatory caution deserves scrutiny. Sati used the historical analogy of the Bund Future migrating overnight from London's open-outcry Life Exchange to the electronic Eurex, where liquidity concentrated and never returned: "This is a Bund future moment where people are like, 'If I don't want to be Life in this case, I need to be Eurex.'"

**Hidden Leverage as the Key Risk**

Asked what keeps him up at night, Sati gave one answer: hidden leverage. Payword has run fully collateralized programs with unified risk engines and near-zero loss ratios for 15 years. His concern is that when leverage is opaque — as it has been systematically in traditional finance, where equity can carry leverage layered multiple times over — risk managers and regulators cannot locate where concentrated exposure actually sits. He frames crypto's transparency as an opportunity to build leverage infrastructure where the full stack is visible rather than compounding in the dark.

**Pope Leo XIV's Encyclical on AI and Crypto**

The episode's final segment addressed Pope Leo XIV's encyclical "Magnifica Humanitas," presented alongside Anthropic co-founder Chris Ola, which named cryptocurrency explicitly alongside AI. The Pope warned against AI as an "instrument of domination, exclusion, and death if unrestrained" and criticized finance that operates without "anthropological and moral foundations" — while affirming that credit funding the real economy "remains irreplaceable."

Perkins observed that the first American pope choosing to address these technologies in a formal teaching document is unambiguously positive for the space's mainstream legitimacy, regardless of the specific critique. His open question: given the encyclical's criticism of financial intermediaries, "maybe the guy likes DeFi."

Sati went further, tracing a philosophical genealogy. Catholic social teaching's subsidiarity principle — decisions at the lowest competent level, power resisting upward concentration — runs through Augustine's "City of God" (worldly power presumed corruptible; stable institutional design must resist concentration) directly to the Bitcoin white paper's argument for monetary subsidiarity at the protocol layer. "The crypto community and the Pope are thinking about the same thing, expressed in different vocabulary," Sati argued, and the political implication is that a decentralized infrastructure argument now carries moral and theological backing for the billion-plus people who follow Catholic teaching.

Campbell extended the genealogy to the American founding: the Federalist Papers' system of checks and balances — executive, legislative, judicial, federal versus state — is a secular version of the same anti-concentration structural argument. He added the pointed historical footnote that the Articles of Confederation failed precisely because they went too far in decentralizing, implying that the right question for crypto is not whether to decentralize but how much.
