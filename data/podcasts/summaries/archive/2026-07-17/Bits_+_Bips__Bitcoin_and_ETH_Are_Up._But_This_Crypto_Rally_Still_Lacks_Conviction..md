# Bits + Bips — "Bitcoin and ETH Are Up. But This Crypto Rally Still Lacks Conviction."

**Host:** Steve Erlic (head of research, Sharplink). **Guest:** Andy Bear (managing director of asset management, GSR; formerly built Coindesk 80 mid-cap crypto index).

## TL;DR

- **June CPI fell to 3.5% from 4.2%** — the market rallied hard on inflation cooling and expectation of delayed rate hikes, but Bear warns this is likely a "booster rocket" relief rally with unclear structural support.
- **"Ambivalence" is the word**: Markets have shown repeated failed rallies (Bitcoin hitting low 80s around Consensus, then dropping to low 60s) that dissipate as quickly as they form, lacking sustained conviction and energy.
- **"Fed solstice" — we don't know peak hawkishness yet** — Bitcoin is sensitive to both inflation expectations and nominal rate expectations; the market won't find conviction until it reaches clarity on where the Fed's rate-hiking cycle peaks.
- **Kevin Worsh as Fed chair brings uncertainty** — He's signaling independence and refusing forward guidance, making it harder for markets to price in Fed actions. His "inflation is a choice" framing is philosophically bold but offers no reassurance on timing.
- **ETF flows whipsawed this week; DATs (digital asset treasuries) are sidelined** — Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs ended 8 weeks of outflows, but flows have been volatile. DATs (which should be structural buyers) are trading below NAV and issuing new shares would be dilutive, so they're not participating in rallies.
- **DeFi rates at risk-free levels signal low leverage appetite** — Lending pools show rates hovering around 3.75%–4.1%, matching Treasury yields. This means low borrowing demand and thus low energy in the market; when DeFi rates spike, it signals traders gearing up to buy.
- **Vaults and fixed-income products maturing in DeFi** — New products like vaults (which bundle DeFi lending pools into yield-bearing tokens) are offering a money-market-like experience on-chain, but they're still nascent and lack standardized yield curves that traditional finance takes for granted.
- **Clarity Act passage would be a surprise; chances fell from 75% to 40%** — Passage by the August 7th deadline is uncertain; the ethics question around Trump's personal crypto holdings is becoming a political sticking point. If it passes, Bear sees genuine upside surprise for crypto prices.

---

**The CPI Rally and Market Ambivalence**

The June CPI reading of 3.5% (down from 4.2% year-on-year) sparked Bitcoin's push toward $65,000 and Ethereum's rise toward $2,000. However, Bear emphasizes that the market's history of failed rallies — including a spike to the low 80s in May around Consensus followed by a sharp drop to the low 60s — reflects a deeper structural issue: the market is still in "ambivalence," not conviction. The relief rally feels powerful in the moment, but without knowing where peak hawkishness lies (what Bear calls the "Fed solstice"), each upswing risks being just a booster rocket that runs out of fuel. Core CPI stayed flat month-over-month for the first time in five years, which is meaningful data, but Fed chair Kevin Worsh has signaled he won't offer the forward guidance that prior chairs (Bernanke, Powell) provided. This lack of guidance makes it harder for markets to feel confident about rally duration.

**The Fed Chair and Rate Expectations**

Worsh's refusal to issue forward guidance or a dot plot is philosophically aligned with Fed independence, but it leaves markets guessing. Steve notes that Worsh's "inflation is a choice" rhetoric is historically significant, but markets are still anxious about whether rates will rise further. Bear points to the distinction between nominal rate expectations and inflation expectations: Bitcoin suffered in 2022 when nominal rates accelerated past inflation expectations (pushing real rates higher), but once markets can clearly see where real rates settle, Bitcoin will have more macro support. The bigger issue is that no one knows when peak hawkishness arrives — without that clarity, even green-light macro data fails to anchor conviction. Worsh faces competing pressures: the administration wants lower rates, but the Fed chair is signaling readiness to tighten if needed.

**ETF Flows and Structural Buyer Gaps**

Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs broke an eight-week streak of outflows with positive inflows this week, a positive sign. However, flows have been whipsawing, and more importantly, traditional structural buyers are absent. Steve notes that Grayscale tapped its ATM for roughly $500 million but didn't buy a single Bitcoin — instead, it's hoarding cash to cover preferred dividend payments. DATs (digital asset treasuries), which should act as steady holders and even buyers during rallies, are trading below NAV. When a DAT's shares trade below NAV, issuing new common shares via an ATM to buy Bitcoin would be dilutive to shareholders, so DATs are largely sitting on the sidelines. Without these structural players, the rally depends on momentum traders and retail energy, which historically hasn't sustained prices through macro uncertainty.

**Reading Energy Through DeFi Rates and Leverage**

Bear has shifted his focus to DeFi lending rates as a leading indicator of market energy. Right now, borrowing rates across major protocols (Morpho, StakeHouse, Gauntlet vaults) are hovering around 3.75%–4.1%, essentially matching the risk-free rate (Treasury yields to one-year). This means there's no credit spread — no one is eagerly borrowing to leverage up and buy crypto. When DeFi rates spike above risk-free rates, it signals that traders are demanding leverage, which historically precedes large price moves. In April, when the market was "super weak" and ambivalent, DeFi rates were depressed. The flat rates now indicate low conviction and low leverage appetite. Bear emphasizes that if Worsh were to surprise the market with an early rate cut (hypothetically), DeFi rates would spike instantly as traders rushed to borrow and loop their spot positions, potentially triggering a real rally with enough energy to pull in ETF flows and DAT participation.

**The Rise of DeFi Fixed Income and Vaults**

While momentum signals remain weak, DeFi is maturing in an unexpected direction: fixed-income products. Vaults are emerging as a key innovation — essentially decentralized money-market funds that bundle together multiple lending pools (Morpho, Aave, Compound, Curve) and emit a yield-bearing token. These are 24/7, globally accessible, and require no traditional fund regulation. However, they're still nascent. Traditional fixed-income markets have standardized yield curves (Treasury curves, swap curves, money-market curves) that allow sophisticated traders to hedge and price risk. DeFi is only beginning to develop these curves. Bear sees this as exciting because it means DeFi is finally playing "the money market game" instead of trying to beat Bitcoin/Ethereum price action. But it also means conviction around fixed-income use cases hasn't yet moved the price needle for crypto assets themselves.

**Leverage and Market Layers**

A robust rally typically has distinct layers, each bringing in new capital. Bear recalls last year's rally (which he views as a textbook example) started with a forced unwinding of a large long-Bitcoin-short-Ethereum hedge-fund position, triggering an Ethereum short squeeze. Then crypto-native traders piled in, followed by spot and perpetual market activity. Finally, ETF inflows arrived, and the Genius Act's passage gave Ethereum an extra boost. This multi-layer structure built real momentum. This year's moves lack those layers. DATs weren't meaningful buyers during last year's rally (they were still new), and they're absent now. ETF inflows exist but are volatile. Without visible leverage (evidenced by rising DeFi rates and perpetual funding rates), each bounce feels isolated rather than part of a self-reinforcing uptrend.

**Conviction and Patience**

Bear's closing advice for potential buyers is pragmatic: wait for a rally with "legs." Trying to catch the absolute bottom during ambivalent markets is psychologically punishing and often results in buying the top of booster-rocket moves. A rally with real conviction — evidenced by rising leverage, ETF inflows, and DAT participation — will feel different and will likely be much more sustainable, even if the entry price is higher than the low. The key threshold is when the market collectively reaches the "Fed solstice" and feels it can see past peak hawkishness. Until then, price moves are likely to reverse quickly.

**Clarity Act and Binary Political Risk**

Steve brings up the Clarity Act, which is being negotiated at the White House as they speak. Passage requires 60 Senate votes, meaning at least 7 Democrats are needed. An earlier Senate Banking Committee version passed on a bipartisan basis, but the two Democratic votes are contingent on resolving ethics language around financial conflicts of interest. The timeline is tight: the bill needs to pass by August 7th to avoid falling into the August recess. Polymarket odds have declined from 75% in May to just under 40% now, suggesting the market views passage as increasingly unlikely. Bear notes that ethics language is becoming a political football because of Trump's disclosures of roughly $1 billion in personal and family crypto holdings. If passage does happen before the deadline, Bear sees it as a genuine surprise that would likely spark upside for crypto prices — not a "buy the rumor, sell the news" situation, because the rumor (passage) has already dissipated from expectations. The surprise itself would be powerful for risk sentiment.

**Why Ambivalence Persists**

Underlying the entire conversation is the absence of predictability. The Fed's new posture of data-reactivity without forward guidance, geopolitical risks (energy prices, Middle East tensions), and domestic political uncertainty (presidential election year dynamics, Treasury liquidity needs) all create an environment where reliable surprise is rare until the "Fed solstice" arrives. Once markets feel confident they've found peak hawkishness and can see the other side, Bear expects the ambivalence to lift "as meaningfully and assertively as it entered." Until then, even positive macro catalysts feel ephemeral.
