# Unchained — "Why Arthur Hayes Thinks the AI Bubble Bursting Could Spark a Crypto Bull Market"

**Host:** Laura Shin. **Guests:** Arthur Hayes (CIO, Maelstrom; founder of BitMEX and inventor of the perpetual swap; author, Cryptohaze Substack).

## TL;DR

- **Iran war inflationary impulse** — War drives spending on AI, drones, supply chain restructuring, and commodity stockpiling globally, triggering central bank credit creation and offsetting deflation from AI labor displacement.
- **Foreign capital flowing out of US assets** — Sovereign nations are reallocating from US Treasury bonds toward physical commodities, pipelines, and refineries after realizing dollar holdings don't secure supply chains during geopolitical shocks (Australia's jet-fuel crisis example).
- **AI bubble catalyst: a catastrophic IPO or merger** — Hayes expects a mega IPO or acquisition so absurdly overvalued that it flops on day one, triggering sector-wide doubt about the capex-to-productivity payoff, similar to how bubbles historically end.
- **Political backlash in 2028 election** — Rising energy costs and construction labor expenses hurt non-stock-owning Americans (90% of population), creating political ammunition for opposition to weaponize as AI narrative ("responsible AI", worker protection), forcing investors to derisk portfolios.
- **AI deflationary bust could rival 2008** — Knowledge workers laid off due to AI efficiency cannot immediately find new jobs; on $40k unemployment insurance vs. $150–200k salaries, they default on consumer credit, creating bank balance sheet holes and forcing central bank stimulus.
- **Hyperliquid's resilient DEX model** — Team achieves authentic organic volume (measured as ADV/OI ratio better than competitors), survives fee competition, and executes token buybacks returning 97% of revenue to holders; hype token rose from $3 to $60 before pullback.
- **Zcash as asymmetric privacy bet** — Hayes holds Zcash as his largest position outside Bitcoin because AI/Big Tech deanonymization will make privacy exponentially more valuable; relative value of Zcash to Bitcoin is "very, very low" and represents asymmetric upside.
- **NEAR protocol gains utility from Zodal** — The Zodal app enables anonymous swaps of any crypto using NEAR intents (shielded Zcash → USDT/Bitcoin), driving fee volume that flips NEAR from inflationary to deflationary as adoption grows.

**Geopolitical Inflation and US Hegemony Erosion**

Hayes argues that wars, especially the US–Iran conflict, are inherently inflationary because they drive military spending, defense innovation (AI, drones), supply chain reconfiguration, and strategic commodity stockpiling. Central banks and commercial banks will create credit to finance these activities, particularly in wealthy developed markets and wealthy non-developed markets. He notes that while peripheral nations like Bangladesh and the Philippines will suffer most from commodity shortages and food crises, developed-market political systems don't prioritize these populations, so markets largely ignore the humanitarian impact.

Hayes also observes a secular shift in how sovereign nations view dollar assets. After the Asian financial crisis in the late 1990s, reserve managers learned to hoard dollars for emergencies. But the Iran war has exposed physical constraints to trade: China halting refined hydrocarbon exports caused Australia—which imports 100% of refined oil from China—to face a jet-fuel crisis. Australia's prime minister flew to Singapore to beg for fuel. This moment crystallized a realization: Treasury bonds and equities don't buy commodities when supply chains break. Sovereign nations are now reconsidering whether to build refineries, pipelines, and commodity reserves instead of holding paper assets. This structural shift away from dollar accumulation means fewer foreign bids for US assets, potentially forcing higher US interest rates and lower equity prices unless the Federal Reserve prints money aggressively.

**The Tan Toll Booth Scenario and Petrodollar Decline**

Hayes notes that one scenario he outlined is now playing out: the "Tan toll booth" in which Iran controls the Strait of Hormuz and extracts payments in yuan and crypto from friendly shipping partners. This effectively undermines the petrodollar system and accelerates the rise of alternative reserve currencies or a currency basket. He does not predict a total US collapse but rather a muddle-through scenario: ceasefire on/off cycles continue, some goods flow, those who can pay the highest prices get them, and over the next one to two years, redundant supply chains and pipelines proliferate, neutralizing Iran's leverage. He frames the US political debate as a tension between those who believe the US should maintain hegemony (requiring expensive military intervention) and those who view empire as a losing financial deal and advocate for power-sharing, though Trump's personal behavior oscillates between both camps.

**Oil Futures as Political Signal**

Hayes uses West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude futures curves as his primary indicator for the war's trajectory and broader economic impact. Trump cares about gas prices because US voters drive extensively and are sensitive to fuel costs. If WTI futures rise sharply, especially in the 6-to-12 month contracts, it signals to the market that the war will persist and worsen. Hayes monitors the spread between front-month and deferred contracts every morning; a widening spread means higher expected prices and longer duration, which worries Trump politically as the November 2024 election approaches (though the episode occurs in May 2026, this reflects Hayes' historical commentary on the mechanism). This metric, Hayes argues, captures what truly matters to political decision-makers regardless of rhetoric.

**Q1 2026 No-Trade Zone and AI Deflation Risk**

Hayes explains his Q1 cautious stance. He feared AI deflation: the top 10–20% of the workforce being displaced by AI tools, losing $100–200k salaries and falling to $40k unemployment insurance, unable to service high consumer debt. This creates bank credit losses. Bitcoin's 2025 price action (decline from October through Q1 relative to software ETFs like IGV) suggested insufficient liquidity creation; central banks seemed confident AI was a productivity miracle requiring no monetary stimulus. Hayes interpreted this as a deflationary risk signal and avoided adding risk in Q1. Now in Q2, he sees inflationary war dynamics and central bank stimulus offsetting that risk, shifting his stance.

**AI Bubble Burst Catalysts and Aftermath**

Hayes identifies two catalysts for AI bubble deflation. First, the "Red Queen" effect (from an economics paper on AI capex): unlike factory capex (which remains useful even if competitors innovate), AI capex is winner-take-all. If you build a data center and your model loses to Claude (as OpenAI did), your capex is worthless. This creates perpetual capex cycles to stay competitive. Eventually a mega-IPO or merger so egregiously valued flops on day one—no buyers, massive selling—signaling the end of the narrative. Second, political backlash: most Americans don't own much stock and are experiencing higher electricity, labor, and construction costs driven by AI data center buildout. The opposition party will weaponize this as a worker-protection narrative, forcing investors to derisk assuming a 50% chance of a Democratic win in 2028 that could bring taxation or regulation crushing AI company valuations.

Hayes contrasts this short-term pain with long-term potential. If humanity uses AI wisely, it could enable robot labor reducing cost of intelligence to near-zero, freeing humans to pursue arts, sports, and leisure instead of corporate jobs. This requires some taxation mechanism on AI/robotic entities to fund a universal or redistributive system, otherwise inequality will be catastrophic. But the current phase—layoffs without immediate job replacement, productivity gains not yet trickling to workers, while costs soar—creates a dangerous window. Banks hold loans from displaced knowledge workers who default, creating balance sheet holes and forcing stimulus. Hayes is confident central banks will print enough to avoid systemic failure, which is his core thesis supporting Bitcoin and crypto.

**Hyperfluid DEX Execution and Organic Volume**

Hayes is bullish on Hyperliquid because its team executed near-perfectly on incentive design and governance. Unlike DYDX and GMX (earlier decentralized perpetuals), Hyperliquid avoided a massive VC presale overhang and structured an aggressive 97% revenue buyback of the HYPE token, aligning token holders directly with platform economics. The team is extremely disciplined about unlocks, only selling ~1% of allowed sales, signaling long-term commitment.

More importantly, Hayes uses the ADV/OI ratio (average daily volume to open interest) as a proxy for organic volume. If the ratio is high, volume is mostly wash trading and liquidity mining noise. Hyperliquid's ratio is the "realest" among competitors because open interest (real capital) grows proportionally with volume. Competitors like Aster, Lighter, and other fee-zero platforms launched to compete but eventually lost volume when incentives dried up. Hyperliquid proved durable, growing from a $3 listing to $60 peak, validating its organic demand.

Hayes also highlights that Hyperliquid enabled weekend price discovery during volatile moments. When Trump makes market-moving announcements Friday night after US market close, Hyperliquid allows anyone with internet access to trade WTI, S&P 500, and NASDAQ perpetuals (undeliverable but price-indicative) on weekend, supplying more information to Monday morning traders. This permissionless access to 7+ billion people outside major Western financial markets represents massive upside for the exchange if volumes scale. As the inventor of the perpetual swap itself (at BitMEX), Hayes takes pride in seeing the product mature at Hyperliquid under Jeff's leadership.

**Privacy Asymmetry with Zcash**

Hayes holds Zcash as his largest non-Bitcoin position because he believes privacy will become exponentially more valuable. AI and Big Tech already deanonymize transactions trivially; ordinary humans underestimate how much smarter AI is than human analysts. As this becomes apparent—years from now—demand for privacy will surge. Zcash has the most robust cryptography, though Monero is a valid alternative. The relative value of Zcash to Bitcoin is currently "very, very low," creating an asymmetric bet. He does not believe Bitcoin should add privacy directly; Bitcoin's strength is its conservative, proven design and public accountability. Privacy coins should be specialized tools, leaving Bitcoin's market share intact.

The Zodal app amplifies this thesis by allowing shielded Zcash holders to swap into any crypto (Bitcoin, USDT on Tron, etc.) via NEAR Protocol intents without revealing the transaction. This direct utility drives fee generation on NEAR, eventually flipping it from an inflationary to deflationary token as volumes grow. For someone seeking privacy, the economic model is compelling: pay a small fee to NEAR intents and keep transactions private.

**DeFi Hack Risks and Governance Tradeoffs**

Hayes acknowledges that on-chain risk is omnipresent and unavoidable. The April hacks (Kelpto and others) reflected poor governance choices: teams opted for less secure but more convenient multisig and contract update mechanisms, and attackers exploited those design decisions. Hayes emphasizes that investors—especially institutional fiduciaries—must ask probing governance questions, not just accept technical jargon. A "one of one" multisig is a red flag. Teams who accept marginally higher operational friction to improve security should be preferred. The solution is not to avoid on-chain assets but to understand the humans and processes behind them.

**Maelstrom Fund Positioning**

Hayes notes he is closing Maelstrom's first anchor investor (name to be disclosed) and deploying in a moment of maximum disillusionment. Founder valuations are much lower than when he first discussed the fund. Institutional investors are either absent (risk-averse) or selling VC positions (overhang). This mirrors the ideal 2023 entry point when he deployed early-stage capital at cycle lows. He expects strong multiples as the market recovers.

**Broader Market Outlook**

Hayes remains confident in his core thesis: central banks will print enough money to prevent systemic failure, supporting Bitcoin, crypto assets, and value creation. Inflation from geopolitical spending and supply chain shock will be the dominant macro force. Token projects with defensible network effects (Hyperliquid's liquidity, Zcash's cryptography, NEAR's intents) and sound tokenomics will benefit. The secular shift from dollar assets to physical reserves and alternative currencies will persist. The AI bubble will eventually burst via a political or market catalyst, but the long-term productivity gains are real. The transition period will be painful for knowledge workers and destabilizing for conventional finance, but crypto's open, permissionless model positions it as an alternative and hedge.
