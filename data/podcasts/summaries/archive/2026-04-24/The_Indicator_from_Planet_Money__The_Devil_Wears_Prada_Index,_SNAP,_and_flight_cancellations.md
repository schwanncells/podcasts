**Flight Cancellations and Surging Fuel Prices**

Adrian Ma reports that Lufthansa Group is canceling 20,000 flights over the next six months in response to dramatically elevated jet fuel costs. The spike stems directly from the ongoing U.S.-Israel war with Iran: the conflict has effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz to most oil shipping and damaged refineries in the Gulf region. As a result, jet fuel prices are up approximately 70% compared to before the war. Major international carriers including Australia's Qantas, Cathay Pacific, and Delta Airlines are all trimming their schedules in response to the surge.

**Demand Destruction in Motion**

The economic term "demand destruction" is being invoked by energy analysts to describe what happens when prices rise high enough that consumers fundamentally change their behavior. When fuel becomes prohibitively expensive, airlines cut flights, drivers drive less, and consumers may choose electric vehicles for their next car. Over the long term, this shift toward alternative fuels could be environmentally beneficial, though in the short term, reduced airline schedules threaten layoffs and create broader economic disruption for airport workers and service staff whose livelihoods depend on flight volume.

**SNAP Participation Collapses After New Law**

Nick Fountain highlights that participation in SNAP, the federal food stamp program, fell by 2.5 million people between July and December 2025, a dramatic decline attributable to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act passed and signed into law in July 2025. Prior to its enactment, lawmakers and policy analysts predicted significant cuts, and the Congressional Budget Office explicitly warned that the changes would result in substantially fewer people on the rolls. The law cut SNAP eligibility for immigrants, veterans, people experiencing homelessness, families with children, and residents of economically depressed areas; it also reduced state funding for administering the program and encouraged states to make it more difficult for people to enroll and remain on the rolls. The results are striking even in these early days, less than a year into the law's implementation. Arizona experienced a particularly severe 47% decrease in SNAP participation, equivalent to 400,000 people, in just the seven months since adoption. While the majority of SNAP recipients are children and adults over 60, the unemployment rate remained steady throughout this period and food inflation continued, suggesting the participation drop is directly attributable to the legal changes rather than improved economic circumstances.

**The Devil Wears Prada Fashion Index**

With The Devil Wears Prada sequel releasing a week after this episode, Waylon examines price inflation on iconic luxury goods from the original 2006 film. A comprehensive analysis of items featured in the movie reveals an average price increase of 79% over the nearly two decades since its release. Anne Hathaway's thigh-high Chanel boots, originally priced at $1,500, now cost over $4,000 for vintage pairs. The Prada tote bag carried by Meryl Streep's character shows even steeper appreciation, climbing from approximately $1,500 in 2006 to nearly $11,000 today. However, not all luxury goods maintain their value equally: a blue leather Marc Jacobs purse that featured prominently in the film fell dramatically from roughly $2,000 to just $150 in vintage condition, illustrating that brand prestige and market demand play critical roles in resale value.

**Wages and Luxury Price Growth**

Despite the apparent runaway inflation in designer goods, wage growth for a specific comparable position outpaced luxury goods inflation. The salary for an assistant to the editor-in-chief at Vogue—the position Anne Hathaway's character fills in the film, based on real Vogue editor Anna Wintour—increased from $32,500 annually in 2006 to $60,000 today, a gain of 85%. This wage increase marginally exceeded the 79% average price increase across the basket of iconic items, suggesting that at least for this elite assistant position, real purchasing power relative to luxury goods has inched upward rather than declined.
