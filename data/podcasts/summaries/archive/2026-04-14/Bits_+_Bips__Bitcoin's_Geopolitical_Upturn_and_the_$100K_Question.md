# Bits + Bips — "Bitcoin's Geopolitical Upturn and the $100K Question"

**Host:** Steve Ehrlich (head of research at Sharplink). **Guests:** Kavita Gutta (founder and general partner, Delta Blockchain Fund).

**The Iran Ceasefire, Oil Prices, and the Crypto Market Reversal**

A tenuous ceasefire between Iran and Israel was agreed on April 7–8 (Thursday/Friday), triggering unusual behavior across crypto and traditional markets. Oil prices fell, and in response, Bitcoin rallied while tech stocks retreated—a sharp reversal from the typical pattern where geopolitical crises send crypto down. Steve Ehrlich notes that prediction markets had signaled the ceasefire correctly hours before official announcements, and that institutions may have been "waiting" to deploy capital rather than trading the daily headlines.

Kavita Gutta emphasizes that this cycle is materially different from past geopolitical shocks. Historically, any political disruption sends crypto down; here, it went up. She attributes this to three forces: first, oil price relief removing an inflation/stagflation risk; second, institutional capital finally entering via Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs after months of withdrawals; and third, Middle Eastern entities transacting in Bitcoin at scale due to banking system dysfunction. She cautions, however, that the ceasefire is fragile—Beirut was bombed mid-ceasefire, Kuwait and Qatar reported explosions, and the Strait of Hormuz remains largely closed. She expects volatility to persist in waves rather than a clean V-shaped recovery.

**The Prediction Market Boom and Insider Trading Ambiguity**

Prediction markets have exploded in liquidity and mainstream attention. Polymarket, Binance prediction markets, Kalshi, and even Robinhood and Coinbase are now integrating prediction-market functionality. During the Iran crisis, insiders and sophisticated traders reportedly made outsized returns by betting on the ceasefire outcome before it was announced.

Steve Ehrlich and Kavita debate the definition of insider trading in prediction markets. In traditional securities, insider trading is illegal because it breaks information symmetry and harms retail investors. Prediction markets, regulated by the CFTC, have no equivalent fiduciary duty or prohibition. Ehrlich notes that US securities law distinguishes between private information you "come across" (not illegal to trade on) versus information tied to your role (illegal). But in unregulated prediction markets, the line blurs. Gutta brings up evidence of Israeli military and defense analysts using prediction markets to infer American strike timing, and Israeli entities engaging in "disinformation betting" to obscure their own intent. She also mentions a Twitter account tracking Pentagon pizza restaurant activity (a signal of imminent military action) to infer when operations are planned.

The problem is systemic. For any prediction—Bad Bunny at the Super Bowl halftime, missile strikes on Iran, policy announcements—someone with inside knowledge will profit. Kalshi has been embroiled in multiple controversies (VP pick, Genius Act timing, Iran missile launches) where betting patterns suggest advance knowledge. The two conclude that prediction markets cannot be regulated away, only managed through transparency and better market design.

**Bitcoin and Ethereum: New Roles, New Relationships**

Bitcoin historically moves first and highest; Ethereum trails by 3–4 days at 70% of Bitcoin's percentage gain; altcoins follow with higher volatility. This cycle has broken that pattern. Ethereum outperformed Bitcoin by 6.7–7% over the 48-hour ceasefire window. Ehrlich and Gutta explore why.

Bitcoin is behaving as a reserve currency at scale—particularly in the Middle East where banking systems have effectively failed. Gutta describes seeing "huge transactions" on Bitcoin at the institutional level in the Middle East, used for supply-chain settlement and cross-border payments. Oil prices falling also released liquidity trapped in gold; some of that wealth is migrating to Bitcoin (reversing the traditional gold-as-safe-haven pattern). Gutta notes that institutional order books on Binance and Coinbase show no large sell walls—unusual for Ethereum after recent rallies—suggesting confidence that the rally will persist.

On ETH specifically, Gutta is encouraged. A Google report warning that quantum computing could break Ethereum's security did not trigger a crash; ETH dipped but held above the 2024 lows of $800–$1,200. The stability signals institutional conviction that Ethereum's technology is solid and the quantum threat is distant. She also notes that ETH, as a "technology token," benefits more from institutional ETF money than Bitcoin does, since institutions view ETH as a tech play.

Gutta forecasts Bitcoin reaching $80k in the next week and $100k+ within a reasonable timeframe. She's less certain on Ethereum, but sees no signs of major sellers queuing up.

**The Breakdown of the Traditional Altcoin Cycle**

For years, crypto market cycles followed a predictable pattern: Bitcoin rallies, then Ethereum, then top-seven altcoins (BNB, Solana, Aptos, etc.), then broader altcoins. This cycle appears to be breaking.

Gutta describes their trading position: Delta Blockchain Fund doesn't day-trade, but looks for good entry points for multi-month/multi-year holds. They've noticed that after Bitcoin and Ethereum moved up, the usual second-wave altcoins (Solana moved a bit, but Aptos, Sui, Monad have stalled) haven't followed. She attributes this to competition from new high-beta vehicles: AI tokens, prediction market plays, 24/7 commodity perps (Hyperliquid's oil futures did over $1 billion daily volume), and tokenized yields on stablecoins. Investors seeking volatility and upside no longer need to wait for altcoins; they can get it elsewhere.

Ehrlich raises the counterpoint: meme coins and newer narratives (he cites pump.fun and the meme-coin phenomenon as "mostly useless" but lucrative for participants). Gutta agrees that narratives recycle (2018–2019 crypto was dead, 2020 DeFi took over, 2021 NFTs boomed), so altcoin cycles may return under new guises. But for now, infrastructure, AI-computational plays (like their portfolio company GMI, which shifted from Bitcoin mining to GPU cloud-compute), and yield-bearing stablecoins are capturing capital.

**Institutional Adoption, Stablecoins, and the Clarity Act**

The past two years have seen major shifts in institutional adoption. Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs have drawn billions (after previous months of outflows). But the growth has been uneven: liquid assets (Bitcoin, ETH, stablecoins for settlement) are thriving; core yield and DeFi products haven't mainstreamed yet.

The Clarity Act—proposed legislation to clarify crypto regulation—has become a focal point. Treasury Secretary Besson published a Wall Street Journal op-ed backing its passage. The bill's centerpiece is enabling yield-bearing stablecoins, which could theoretically compete with Treasury bills and bank deposit accounts by offering 6–8% APY.

The banking lobby has mounted two arguments against yield stablecoins: first, that they'll drain liquidity from traditional banks (especially community/regional banks that rely on deposit bases); second, that not all stablecoins meet the same standards, so opening the door to yield stablecoins could lead to regulatory arbitrage and fraud.

Gutta dismisses both as deflections. On the first: the banking system's problem is its own inefficiency; regulators shouldn't handicap innovation to protect bad actors. On the second: the solution is standardization (via regulation or market choice), not prohibition. Banks already manage risky deposits (they use FDIC insurance); stablecoins can do the same by enforcing collateralization and audit standards.

Ehrlich's read is that odds of Clarity passage depend on the exact text. If Congress defines a regulatory box for stablecoins, passes it through the legislature (not just SEC/CFTC rulemaking), and gets a Senate vote, passage is likely and market-positive. But if the final bill includes major carve-outs or restrictions (e.g., prohibiting yield entirely or limiting participants), it becomes a hold-the-line victory for entrenched finance.

**Geopolitical Fragility and the Oil Price Wild Card**

Both speakers return to geopolitics as the macro wild card. Oil prices, not policy or Fed rates, drove the crypto rally. Gutta explicitly warns that she doesn't trust the US is "out of the war"—Beirut bombing mid-ceasefire, multiple reports of Israeli and Iranian strikes within the Gulf region. If the conflict re-escalates and oil spikes again, crypto will face headwinds.

She also notes that the Strait of Hormuz remains largely closed, limiting oil flows even if geopolitical tensions ease. A full recovery to $60–$70/barrel oil requires not just ceasefire, but reopening of shipping channels. That could take weeks or months.

**Future Frontiers: Crypto-AI-Robotics**

Looking ahead, Gutta highlights the convergence of crypto, AI, and robotics as the highest-conviction bet for 2026–2027. Unlike the 2024 AI-crypto hype cycle (which saw valueless projects raise millions then disappear), this wave is revenue-driven from day one. Examples include: decentralized identity systems for autonomous AI agents, AI trading bots settling trades on-chain, robot-as-service companies recording their identity and transactions on public blockchains.

She notes that infrastructure companies (like their portfolio company GMI, which pivoted from mining to GPU cloud compute) are making the transition. The next phase is not financial products, but industrial infrastructure: robots managing supply chains, AI agents negotiating contracts on-chain, decentralized identity replacing OAuth.

This shift would move crypto from a purely financial instrument (which competes with traditional finance) to an infrastructural layer (which complements both Web2 and Web3). That, she argues, is where the long-term value is.

**Conclusion and Key Signals to Watch**

Gutta's top signals for Q2–Q3 2026:
- Ethereum as the canary: if ETH moves, alts follow. If ETH stalls, the broader market is likely consolidating.
- Major IPOs: Kraken, Falcon X, and possibly OpenAI or Anthropic. Liquidity from tech IPOs has historically flowed into crypto.
- Oil prices: the true macro barometer. If oil rises above $80, crypto faces headwinds; if it stays below $70, bullish.

She expects crypto to remain volatile for the next 6–12 months due to geopolitical instability, and sees no "very stable" policy environment. But she's constructive on the long term: institutional adoption is real, stablecoin yield is coming (with Clarity or without), and the crypto-AI-robotics synergy is beginning to show real product-market fit.
