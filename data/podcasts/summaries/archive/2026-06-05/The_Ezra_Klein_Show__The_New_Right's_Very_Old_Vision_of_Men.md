# The Ezra Klein Show — "The New Right's Very Old Vision of Men"

**Host:** Ezra Klein. **Guest:** Helen Lewis (staff writer, The Atlantic; author of *Difficult Women: A History of Feminism in Eleven Fights* and *The Genius Myth*; recent author of Atlantic cover story mapping contemporary "masculinism" on the New Right).

## TL;DR

- **"Masculinism" as unifying ideology:** Lewis argues that across splits on war, trade, and Israel, MAGA unity coalesces around the claim that feminism went too far and traditional gender roles are natural—a shared belief that transcends other policy divisions.
- **Bronze Age Pervert's hormonal politics:** Costin Alamariou (Romanian Yale-educated internet persona) frames testosterone decline as the core driver of modernity's failures, arguing low-T creates a "docile" man unsuited for hierarchy, dominance, and civilization—a narrative that maps neatly onto MAGA masculinity despite lacking historical evidence.
- **Helen Andrews' "Great Feminization" thesis:** Andrews argues wokeness is an epiphenomenon of workforce feminization (women in law, academia, HR), but Lewis finds the causation argument collapses under scrutiny—bureaucratic dysfunction and cancellation predate women's entry, and social media algorithms drive both, not gender demographics.
- **Policy proposals emerging:** Rollback of no-fault divorce, tax incentives for traditional families (Heritage Foundation), household voting (Doug Wilson), exclusion of women from combat roles (Pete Hegseth), and leveraging equal opportunity mechanisms to pursue reverse-discrimination claims—all moving through governance, not just rhetoric.
- **Self-help severed from purpose:** The movement sells bodybuilding and "looks-maxing" as self-improvement but decoupled from genuine flourishing—examples like streamer Andrew Tate and influencer Clavicular show the pursuit of attraction becoming pathological, infertility-inducing obsession divorced from meaningful relationships or community.
- **Women recruited, then discarded:** Sam Adler-Bell's piece documents right-wing women influencers who aligned with the movement, believing promises of respect and traditionalism, only to discover the underlying misogyny applied to them too—a pattern Lewis connects to Kate Manne's theory of misogyny as selectively exempting "good girls."
- **Left's aesthetic and futurism gap:** MAGA has a cohesive visual identity and draws from the past for aesthetic authority; the left struggles to articulate beauty, skepticism of technology, and weak alternative to what these movements offer on community, ritual, meaning—a genuine vulnerability Lewis identifies.

**"Masculinism" as the New Right's Unifying Ideology**

Lewis describes her Atlantic piece as mapping an ideology she calls "masculinism"—distinct from the manosphere (Andrew Tate, incels, algorithm-creatures) because it has institutional backing, think-tank presence, and political ambition. She argues it has become a unifying theory for MAGA that transcends typical right-wing fractures. While the coalition splits on Iran war, Israel, and protectionism, ask anyone whether feminism went too far and you get near-consensus: traditional gender roles are better, equality has been a failed pursuit, and empathy (coded as feminine) has corrupted politics by creating sympathy for "underdogs" (violent criminals, illegal immigrants). This coherence around gender grievance is what makes it potent.

**Bronze Age Pervert and the Hormonal Theory of Politics**

Ezra walks through Costin Alamariou's (aka Bronze Age Pervert, BAP) book *Bronze Age Mindset*, which begins with real phenomena—measurable testosterone decline in men, falling sperm quality across countries, fertility rate collapse in liberal democracies. But BAP then argues the core of masculinity is testosterone ("thymus"), and modernity is systematically destroying it through endocrine-disrupting chemicals, bad diet, and emasculating institutions. The book presents this as the "fundamental fact of nature: inequality." BAP's political valence is explicit: high-T men are risk-taking and don't mind inequality; liberal democracy is inherently feminine because it prioritizes preventing harm to the weak and equality. Ezra notes the leap is dramatic—from biological observation to claims that men cannot be fully men in liberal democracy—yet the book ends with practical advice to throw out plastic cutting boards and filter water. Lewis finds the contradiction telling: these are people who have never experienced real material deprivation yet fetishize danger and hierarchy. She connects it to Fukuyama's prediction that people sated with safety would grow bored and seek artificial thrills.

**The Literary and Political Genealogy**

Lewis places BAP and others (Charles Cornish Dale, Scott Yenor, Doug Wilson) in a genealogy of Nietzsche, paganism, and Christian nationalism—though without agreement on *when* the golden age was. Some want 1950s suburbia, others Salem 1650, others Roman/Spartan hierarchies, others pre-modern pagan vitalism. The incoherence is striking: one can critique modernity identically at the end of the 19th century (as Nietzsche did) in a world without antibiotics, yet make the same argument now. Lewis calls out the "longhouse" essay by Lomas as exemplary: it invokes a matriarchal communal system as a cautionary tale, explicitly states no specific historical referent exists, then refuses to define it "lest it lose its force to lampoon." This is trolling dressed as political philosophy—serious argument smuggled in gift-wrapped in irony.

**The Troll's Grammar and Rejection of Empiricism**

Ezra and Lewis discuss how the offensiveness is strategic. Using slurs creates an in-group ("you're cool with this like us guys") and traps opponents at the level of language rather than idea—say "I want to sterilize retards" and listeners focus on the word, not the eugenic policy beneath. These writers explicitly reject fact-based argumentation as too bloodless; they claim deeper truths live in vibes, aesthetics, and myth. This frustrates Lewis, who spent a decade arguing feminism against claims that feminists "just talk feelings"—now the right says facts don't matter, only vibes. Yet the actual arguments fall apart under scrutiny. BAP claims young men have low testosterone because modernity, but young men have much higher testosterone than old men, so older men shouldn't lead either—a claim they don't make. The universality collapses.

**Helen Andrews' "Great Feminization" and Its Flaws**

Andrews' viral essay argues wokeness is an epiphenomenon of workforce feminization: as women entered law, academia, and administration, these fields allegedly collapsed into "bitching and backbiting" because women's lack of thymus makes them feel-driven rather than rigor-driven. In law, she claims, women let criminals off; in academia, no hard questions, just kumbaya circles. Ezra and Lewis trace the claim: Andrews points to Larry Summers' ouster as a key "feminist freakout" moment. But Lewis investigated and found Summers' private emails to Jeffrey Epstein revealed far worse views on gender than his public comments; his Harvard colleagues likely used the controversy as convenient cover to remove someone they already wanted out. Moreover, in 2006 when Summers was fired, four-fifths of Harvard's tenured faculty were men. The feminization claim doesn't hold. On cancellation, Andrews argues it's a feminine punishment (vs. male physical violence), so cancellation's rise reflects women taking over. But Lewis notes cancellation is an outcome of social media algorithms, not gender—the Trump administration weaponized firings for "diversity" language, Senator McCarthy conducted purges without women present, ostracism existed in ancient Athens before women had rights.

**The Ubermensch Problem and Veil of Ignorance**

The books invoke Nietzsche's claim that nature is fundamentally hierarchical and unequal; liberals are deluded to think otherwise. BAP writes that "the many find solace in meaning only in submission," and hierarchy held by "command and obedience" is natural and good. Ezra pushes back: these authors assume *they* would be Cleopatras or Roman citizens, not toothless peasants or enslaved people. He invokes Rawls' "veil of ignorance"—would you prefer to be any random Roman or any random American today? Nearly everyone picks modern America. Yet the implicit belief is that the author is special, thwarted by modernity. Lewis sees it as elite overproduction (Peter Turchin's thesis)—people with intellectual ambitions who didn't fit into university hierarchies and now find an outlet in the new right's intellectual framing.

**Healthy Critiques and a Better Vision of Masculinity**

Lewis acknowledges genuine problems worth naming: boys do struggle in schools designed for passive girls, ADHD diagnoses affect boys differently, testosterone/sperm quality *is* declining and worth investigating, men's wages have stagnated, male friendship networks have collapsed, male suicide is rising. But she rejects the competitive framing ("girls are winning") and points to capitalism harming both—girls face Instagram body dysmorphia, boys face crypto/day-trading traps. The left needs to make space for conversations about boys' real problems without them devolving into "childless cat ladies did this to you."

Ezra counters with a vision of masculinity rooted in self-mastery and discipline—channeling strength, competitiveness, and aggression in pro-social directions, toward obligation to others and children. He's struck by how little fatherhood appears in these books. The books promise vitality and excellence but deliver anxiety, surveillance, and resentment. Lewis loves qualities in men that these texts ignore: obsessive nerd-focus, humor, comfort with imperfection, warmth. Her marriage of equals is "so much more relaxing" than the control-and-disobedience model these texts envision. The visions are "so fucking warped," deeply depressing, and "a terrible vision of what it means to be an adult."

**The Eugenics Problem**

Lewis points to the eugenic genealogy: Buck v. Bell language about the "unfit," physical perfection as moral truth, inherited hierarchies as natural. BAP's fixation on athletic physiques, his contempt for the obese ("yeasty"), parallels 20th-century eugenics that sterilized thousands in California and elsewhere. His explicit anti-Semitism (Jews as pallid, nerdy, disconnecting people from vital forces) is "very old-fashioned anti-Semitism." The movement's obsession with bodybuilding and "looks-maxing" creates mass body dysmorphia in young men—Clavicular, the mega-streamer, is infertile from stacked testosterone, can't date, yet remains a role model. The urge to be attractive has been severed from its purpose (connection, relationship) and become pathological.

**Real Proposals and Policy Pathways**

These aren't just ideas—some are moving through governance. Heritage Foundation's 2025 report proposes tax incentives for traditional families, opposition to dating apps and daycare. Doug Wilson wants household voting (husband votes for household) and women excluded from combat (they're "life givers, nurturers"). Pete Hegseth (Defense Secretary) tweeted support for Wilson's attacks on women's suffrage and wants an army without beards. The EOC chair solicits white male discrimination claims like an ambulance chaser. Scott Yenor proposes male-only military colleges. The Dobbs decision already overturned Roe. Most radical proposals (repeal of the 19th Amendment) won't happen, but as with abortion, the strategy is incremental: regulatory capture, tax code tweaks, bureaucratic burden-piling. Ezra notes the American system is friendly to minoritarian ideas—you don't need a referendum if you control state houses and obscure bureaucracies.

**Women's Complicity and Disillusionment**

Sam Adler-Bell's piece in New York Magazine documents right-wing women influencers who bought the pitch—they didn't like what they saw as left-wing "schoolmarmishness" or shared conservative views. They played along, even argued for the vision. Then discovered the men around them were cruel, and the promised traditionalism where they'd be cherished was just cover for disrespect. Lewis connects this to Ariel Levy's *Female Chauvinist Pigs*—women adopting male-dominated norms to claim they're "honorary guys," only to realize the misogyny applied to all women, including them. Kate Manne's theory of misogyny promises exemptions for "good girls" who behave—cross an invisible tripwire and the exemption vanishes. This is the half-irony problem: you think it's ironic sexism in a feminized gynocracy, then discover it's unironic.

**The Deeper Question: Community, Ritual, Meaning**

Ezra raises the spiritual question: these ideas gain traction because they fill real voids. Modern life is hollow, institutions feel empty, and modernity alienates us from immediacy and authenticity. For people without religion, you lose communal rhythm (Palm Sunday, Easter, Advent, confession, funerals)—anchors that structured life. These movements promise meaning, community, ritual through beauty, hierarchy, and shared struggle. The left has offered little in return and abandoned kids into social media farms where extremism thrives unchecked. Lewis agrees the root is real but worries about the solution: self-help severed from genuine flourishing. Following this ideology would make you worse, not better. She sees it partly as a failure of the mainstream to offer a vision of human flourishing that *feels vital*.

The two discuss how social media—a dopamine drip for billions—has created sex-segregated algorithmic feeds where young women trend left and young men trend right, without real-world mixing (playgrounds, youth centers, pools) where Alice Evans' research suggests de-radicalization happens naturally through proximity.

**Aesthetics and the Left's Vulnerability**

Ezra notes the movement takes aesthetics seriously—from Trump's concern with how people and spaces look to BAP's insistence that aesthetic physique has cosmic significance. This maps onto a real hunger: modernity lacks beauty, politics abandoned aesthetic opinion. MAGA has a coherent visual identity; the left does not. Kamala Harris doesn't have an "aesthetic." The far left (blue-fringed, Palestine plushies) does, but mainstream Democrats don't. Zeran Mamdani's campaign had colors and consistency; Obama's had the hope posters. Trump's aesthetic runs through architecture, museum content, classical beauty. It works because the movement is backward-looking—you can pick a past aesthetic and claim it. The left can't as easily reach backward without appearing to endorse the past's harms. Hamilton tried (90s nostalgia filtered through multicultural update) but it's hard. Gavin Newsom could run on California's future-building (new skyscrapers) but Americans are pessimistic—anxious about jobs, living standards, climate. Waymos seem cool to Ezra, but taxi drivers' unions oppose them. The left is skeptical of tech (AI widened that skepticism), so it can't claim a sci-fi/techno-punk aesthetic.

Trump's aesthetic is "normie taste with money"—gold everything, like a regular person who suddenly got rich, not elite restraint (muted tones). This appeals to regular people who see his lack of elite taste as validation that normal things are nice. The left's problem is partly hatred of the "middle brown" and the mainstream. Ezra wishes Democrats had no shame about earnestness (Hamilton's cringe now, but it was hopeful). Lewis notes the best politics are cringe because they're earnest—and having no sense of cringe is a useful asset in American politics.

**The Risk and Opportunity**

The closing argument: the right now controls Twitter's fever swamp. Nick Fuentes says women should be "put in gulags" like Hitler did. Doug Wilson condemned Fuentes' language yet also demurs that Fuentes is a "Fed" (a conspiracy theory while Trump controls the government). The movement built itself on "don't be like the PC ladies who won't say bad words," yet can't enforce any lines against their own extremes—you made politics about having no lines, so how enforce any? The anti-Semitism stuck (Fuentes got platform grief for it), but misogyny didn't because "there were enough powerful people in the coalition" who went "this is our line." So they drew one around anti-Semitism but not women.

The left has an opportunity if it can offer appealing politics—the right has driven itself partly crazy, with key figures constantly offering terrible things and offensive associations. But if Trump faces inflation and gas price spikes, voters won't care about culture war messaging. The test is whether the left can meet the real alienation and ennui with something healthier, more virtuous, more ambitious.

**Books Recommended**

Lewis recommends three works: *Christy Marlowe's Own Double Entry* by B.S. Johnson (young alienated man discovers double-entry bookkeeping—for every slight done to him, he enacts one on society, capturing alienation). Nancy Mitford's biography of Madame de Pompadour (portrait of Versailles during Louis XV, an elite with no idea the guillotine approaches—sparkling anthropological study of disconnected elites). *The Genius Factory* by David Plotz (eugenic millionaire convinces Nobel Prize winners to donate sperm; doesn't go well; includes William Shockley, who later became a racial IQ theorist—a California story of sperm, entrepreneurship, and eugenics).
