**The Fed's Hawkish Turn Under Kevin Warsh**

The FOMC held rates at 3.5–3.75% at Kevin Warsh's first meeting as Fed chair, but removed the easing bias from the statement — a meaningful shift in signaling. Nine of 18 officials now write down a rate hike in their projections. Pandl notes that Warsh is more data-driven than Jerome Powell and issued a notably brief statement by comparison; Powell remains on the board as a regular Fed Governor. May CPI came in at 4.2%, with oil prices as a partial driver tied to the Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz ceasefire. The combination of persistent inflation and a newly hawkish Fed creates a more challenging rate environment than markets had priced in earlier in the year.

**Equities: AI Euphoria and the SpaceX Float Squeeze**

Pandl expresses caution on equities, citing rising real rates and concern about AI valuations. He also points to the degree of inter-cross-investment among major tech firms — meaning their balance sheets are highly correlated with each other, concentrating risk in ways that may not be fully appreciated. The SpaceX IPO served as an illustration of structural dynamics in modern markets: with only a 4.2% float, the stock surged approximately 50% in a short-term squeeze. Pandl highlights that Hyperliquid's perpetuals market accurately priced SpaceX before the IPO, demonstrating that crypto-native trading infrastructure can reflect price discovery ahead of traditional markets. Tokenized equity structures that could democratize access to pre-IPO companies received an "incomplete grade" — the concept is viable but regulatory clarity is still missing.

**Crypto Winter: Strategy's Dividend Problem**

Pandl describes the current environment as a "conventional crypto winter" — roughly 50% declines across digital assets, with a duration he expects to be longer than typical. Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy) presents a specific case study in the strains that creates. Saylor sold 32 BTC on June 1, then turned around and bought 1,587 BTC two weeks later — the asymmetry illustrating ongoing accumulation even amid pressure. The preferred shares (STRCH) are trading below $91 against a target of approximately $100, and Pandl estimates roughly 7 months of dividend runway remain at current cash levels. His prescription: Strategy should use options and derivatives to generate income and cover dividend obligations rather than rely solely on BTC appreciation. He observes that retail investors are pulling back because the product is delivering more Bitcoin beta than they expected — the risk profile was not fully communicated or understood.

**Gold vs. Bitcoin: Separating Fundamentals from Speculation**

Gold finished its worst quarter in years, while Bitcoin showed strength early in the Iran war — though Pandl attributes that move to oversold conditions reverting rather than a durable new macro bid. He draws a clear line through gold's price history: the move from $2,000 to $4,000 was driven by fundamentals — central banks repatriating gold from London and New York vaults, a trend that accelerated after the Russia sanctions in 2022 restructured the geopolitical case for holding gold outside the Western financial system. The additional leg from $4,000 to $5,500 was speculative excess, and that excess is now washing out. Pandl argues both gold and Bitcoin "look pretty good here" because both are anchored to the same fundamental driver: fiscal deficit and sovereign debt imbalances that erode confidence in fiat currency. The assets are complements rather than competitors in that framing.

**DeFi and Alts: The Revenue Thesis Takes Hold**

Pandl identifies the revenue story as the organizing theme for crypto assets in the second half of the year, and Hyperliquid is his central example. The protocol returns cash flows directly to token holders, making it possible to apply a conventional DCF valuation — a significant shift from earlier crypto cycles where value accrual was speculative and indirect. Grayscale published a DCF-based price target for Aave, extending the same analytical framework to a lending protocol. Standard Chartered issued a buy rating on Uniswap. Pandl argues that regulatory clarity combined with clear revenue mechanics is what institutional investors are waiting for, and those conditions are increasingly being met in DeFi even as broader crypto markets remain in a winter.
