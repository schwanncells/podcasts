# Unchained — "Why Kalshi's John Wang Says Perps Are 'the Most Pure Trading Instrument'"

**Host:** Laura Shin. **Guest:** John Wang (head of crypto, Kalshi; led Kalshi's push to bring the first CFTC-regulated crypto perpetual futures to the US market).

## TL;DR

- **Kalshi launched the first US on-shore crypto perps** — the only CFTC-regulated domestic perps exchange on launch day, tapping a global market Wang describes as $90 trillion in annual volume.
- **Perps are "the most pure trading instrument"** — no expiry date means no contract rollover costs; leverage amplifies exposure; positions can profit from both rising and falling prices and be held indefinitely.
- **Leverage is set by Kalshi's risk engine, not chosen freely** — Bitcoin maxes at 6x, ETH at 4.4x, HYPE at 2.1x, with specific caps per asset determined by historical volatility, liquidity, and CFTC-reviewed models.
- **CME CEO Terry Duffy called perps "a disaster waiting to happen"** — Wang counters that CME's own futures products carry higher leverage than Kalshi's perp offering, and Kalshi operates within the same CFTC regulatory framework.
- **Institutions are already using Kalshi for hedging** — Arca and Galaxy executed block trades priced on Kalshi's Clarity Act odds; bars in New York hedge NBA Finals revenue; integration via FCMs and prime brokerage terminals enables hedge fund access.
- **Insider trading protections include KYC + IC360 partnership** — Kalshi maintains banned-entity lists covering athletes, coaches, politicians, and campaign staff, and monitors for retrospective suspicious patterns.
- **Kalshi raised $1 billion at a $22 billion valuation** in May, led by Coatue.
- **Polymarket rivalry dismissed** — Wang says the perps launch "announcement" was a journalist leak, not an intentional undercut; he declines to engage with alleged spying claims.

**Why Kalshi Launched Crypto Perps**

Kalshi's prediction market business is its core, but crypto has become the second-largest category on the platform, reaching roughly $3 billion per month in volume — up approximately 15x since the start of 2026. Wang positions perpetual futures as a natural extension: like prediction markets, perps trace their origin to academic economists, represent a financial instrument with demonstrated demand, and had never been available to US traders in a regulated, on-shore form. Kalshi was the only domestic exchange with CFTC approval on launch day.

**What Perps Are and How They Work**

Wang calls perps "the most pure trading instrument" because they let a trader take a directional position on any asset — long or short — without an expiration date. Traditional futures and options force traders to roll over contracts at expiration, paying re-entry costs each time; perps eliminate that friction. Leverage is built in, allowing traders to hold exposure larger than their posted collateral. Wang distinguishes the offering from offshore venues like Hyperliquid by emphasizing the regulated, KYC-required US structure, simpler onboarding, and in-product tools like stop-loss and take-profit guardrails that guide retail users.

**Leverage, Pricing, and Risk Models**

Leverage limits are not round numbers: 6x on Bitcoin, 4.4x on ETH, 2.1x on HYPE. These are outputs of Kalshi's risk engine, calibrated to each asset's historical volatility, return profile, and liquidity scenarios, and reviewed by the CFTC. Index pricing comes from CF Benchmarks, a regulated benchmark provider that aggregates prices from multiple CFTC-regulated US exchanges using a proprietary 30-page methodology designed to be tamper-resistant. For prediction markets on the same infrastructure, Kalshi uses a 60-second time-weighted average.

On risk protection, Kalshi's system differs from offshore exchanges through a multi-layered margin model, a guarantee fund backed by exchange members and Kalshi itself, and segregated customer accounts — all requirements of operating as a CFTC-regulated venue. Wang says back-tested scenarios have not triggered auto-deleveraging, and the guarantee fund is the first line of defense when liquidations go awry, rather than the Hyperliquid model of closing the most profitable positions first.

**CME's Criticism and Competitive Position**

CME CEO Terry Duffy publicly called crypto perps "a disaster waiting to happen," citing extreme leverage and automatic liquidations as threats to retail investors. Wang's response: Kalshi's maximum leverage is lower than the leverage available on CME's own futures products, and both exchanges operate under the same CFTC framework. The argument is that Kalshi is not introducing something categorically riskier than what already exists — it is bringing an already-large global instrument into the regulated domestic market.

On the competitive landscape, Wang views Hyperliquid as serving a distinct user base — on-chain, no KYC, crypto-native — while Kalshi targets US retail and institutions who want a regulated interface. Other entrants include Coinbase (pre-existing perp-like product), Kraken (acquired Bitnomial for CFTC access), Robinhood, and Gemini. Wang frames this as a "positive-sum" market where adoption is at "inning zero" — roughly 0.2–0.5% of the US has used perpetual futures.

**Asset Selection and Regulatory Pathway**

Kalshi launched with Bitcoin and has since added ETH, SOL, XRP, DOGE, HYPE, SUI, LINK, Bitcoin Cash, Shiba Inu, and Litecoin. New assets within the approved crypto class can be self-certified and listed quickly, subject to building risk models and informal CFTC review. Expanding to other asset classes — stocks, indices, commodities — requires a formal CFTC 40.3 filing for each new class. Agricultural commodity perps will not be permitted. Wang declined to name which new asset classes are in progress; equity perps would also involve the SEC.

**Institutional Use Cases**

Institutional uptake is visible in at least one high-profile public case: Arca, Galaxy, and an undisclosed counterparty executed large block trades hedging their portfolios using Kalshi's Clarity Act odds, which have fluctuated substantially. Wang also cites small-business use cases — bars in New York hedging NBA Finals revenue, watchmakers hedging World Cup outcomes — as early signs of real hedging demand. Kalshi has integrated with FCMs, prime brokerages, and trading terminals, allowing hedge funds to access the platform without changing existing workflows.

**Insider Trading Controls and the Polymarket Rivalry**

Kalshi's KYC requirement is a baseline protection that offshore prediction markets lack. Kalshi additionally partners with IC360, a data provider for sports leagues, to maintain lists of athletes, coaches, team staff, and political figures — all of whom are pre-emptively blocked from trading related markets. Retrospective pattern detection supplements this. Wang contrasts this with the insider trading vulnerability of no-KYC venues.

On Polymarket, Wang declines to engage substantively with claims that Kalshi has "planted smear stories" or spied on Polymarket's roadmap. He addresses the perps timing allegation specifically: the launch was leaked by a journalist, he says, not intentionally announced to preempt Polymarket's own planned announcement hours later.
