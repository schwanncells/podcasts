# The Indicator from Planet Money — "Is anyone gonna do anything about these Iran War trades?"

**Hosts:** Waylon Wong, Ricky Mulvey. **Guests:** Michael Koh (trader, Tidal Financial Group); Kristen Johnson (former CFTC commissioner, 2022–2025, serving under both Biden and Trump); Steven Peepgrass (partner, Troutman Pepper Lock).

## TL;DR

- **Two suspiciously timed trades — a $1.5 billion S&P 500 long and a few hundred million dollar oil short — were placed on the morning of March 23rd**, minutes before Trump posted on Truth Social about "very good and productive conversations" on Middle East peace.
- **The trades netted roughly $50 million within a very short period** as the S&P rose and oil fell on the news.
- **The "fishy" elements:** size, the two bets placed one right after the other, and the fact that the catalyst was entirely off-calendar — Trump's ceasefire hint was unscheduled and unexpected.
- **The CFTC could easily identify the traders** via know-your-customer records at the exchange, but its Division of Enforcement was cut under DOGE, leaving fewer investigators to pursue cases.
- **The CFTC is investigating the oil trade**; it declined to comment further.
- **Prediction markets like Kalshi and Polymarket introduce a harder problem** — the international version of Polymarket checks location, not ID, uses crypto, and makes identifying traders "very difficult, if not impossible."
- **An anonymous Polymarket account ("Maga My Man") correctly bet on the timing of U.S. strikes on Iran** and has made hundreds of thousands of dollars; the SDNY has opened a preliminary investigation into prediction-market insider trading, with CFTC cooperation.

**The Suspicious Futures Trades**

On the morning of March 23rd, two large futures bets appeared in quick succession: a $1.5 billion wager that the S&P 500 would rapidly rise, and a separate bet of a few hundred million dollars that oil prices would fall. Both were outsized relative to other trades that morning. Minutes later, Trump posted on Truth Social — in all-caps — that there had been "very good and productive conversations regarding a complete and total resolution of our hostilities in the Middle East." The S&P rose; oil fell. The trades were up roughly $50 million in a very short period. Iranian state media later disputed that any talks had taken place, but the positions had already paid off.

Michael Koh, a trader with Tidal Financial Group, flagged the trades as suspicious on three grounds: the sheer scale, the fact that the two bets followed each other in immediate sequence, and — most critically — that Trump's announcement was "not specifically on the calendar," making the pre-announcement positioning hard to explain as routine market activity.

**What Regulators Can and Can't Do**

Former CFTC commissioner Kristen Johnson noted that traditional futures exchanges operate under "know your customer" rules, meaning that a simple inquiry letter to the exchange would produce account information quickly. The CFTC has the legal and technical ability to identify who made these trades. The agency is in fact investigating the oil trade.

The complication is capacity. Johnson described a significant restructuring of the CFTC's Division of Enforcement under DOGE-era cuts, leaving the agency with fewer investigators. She acknowledged that large correlated bets could theoretically result from algorithmic herding — one big position triggering copycat trades — but said trades that appear to reflect access to confidential or classified military information raise "red flags almost immediately" and warrant a hard look.

**Prediction Markets: The Regulatory Wild West**

Kalshi is fully regulated by the CFTC, having gone through the legal process to operate as a futures market in the U.S. Polymarket is split: one side is seeking full CFTC regulation; the other is an unregulated international platform that uses cryptocurrency and checks users' location but not their identity. Attorney Steven Peepgrass characterized this as "the Wild West" and noted that identifying traders on the offshore crypto platform can be "very difficult, if not impossible."

The stakes extend beyond money. On Polymarket International, users can bet on whether Iran will have a nuclear weapon by end of 2026, or whether Kharg Island will be struck within weeks. Peepgrass flagged two distinct national security risks: someone with classified knowledge could tip off the market through their bets, and someone with money riding on an outcome who also has influence over events creates an obvious conflict.

**The Maga My Man Account and Regulatory Response**

An anonymous Polymarket account called "Maga My Man" has made hundreds of thousands of dollars, including a correct bet on the timing of U.S. strikes on Iran. Adversaries could theoretically monitor accounts like this for signals about U.S. military intentions. The White House reportedly sent staff an email warning against using insider information to bet on prediction markets. The U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York has begun a preliminary investigation into prediction-market insider trading, with CFTC cooperation. Where it leads remains unknown.
