# The Indicator from Planet Money — "Obsession, the most fun job in China, and a new green card policy"

**Host:** Wailin Wong, Darian Woods, Adrian Ma. **Guests:** None.

**Shepherd Jobs and China's Labor Market Anxiety**

The episode opens with Darian Woods' indicator: 700 people applied for two shepherd positions in rural China. The jobs drew international attention via Weibo, signaling deep anxiety about China's job market despite an official unemployment rate of 5.2%. The applicants included people with university degrees—remarkable for pastoral work. One applicant told Reuters he had been working in a shipping container factory, fastening screws for over 13 hours per day. The shepherd positions, located south of Mongolia, paid above the national urban average for private companies and included food and board. However, the conditions were demanding: winter temperatures drop to minus 22 degrees Fahrenheit, and the job involved managing 3,000 sheep. The farm owner ultimately hired two couples with actual farm experience rather than city-dwellers or degree-holders, recognizing that relevant work history mattered more than credentials.

**The Viral Horror Film with YouTube Roots**

Wailin Wong presents her indicator: a 39% jump in box office ticket sales between opening weekend and the second weekend for the horror movie Obsession. This is statistically rare—most wide-release films see box office decline after opening weekend. The film was written and directed by Cray Barker, who started as an aspiring actor making comedy videos with a friend, built a large YouTube following, then made a full-length horror film and released it free on YouTube. The film caught attention at the Toronto International Film Festival, leading to a theatrical distributor. Made for under $1 million, it has grossed over $60 million so far. The story of Obsession involves a young man who makes a wish on a supernatural toy that his crush will love him more than anyone else in the world—a dark wish that escalates into horror. Adrian Ma notes that another horror film, Backrooms, opening that week was also directed by a young creator known for making web videos, suggesting YouTube and web creators are becoming a fertile training ground for theatrical horror filmmakers.

**Green Card Policy Shift and Uncertainty**

Adrian Ma's indicator is 1.4 million—the number of immigrants who received green cards in 2024. Most green cards go annually to immigrants already living in the U.S. who apply for "adjustment of status." These may be former student visa holders who married a U.S. citizen, or people on work visas who decided to stay. The Trump administration has announced a new U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services policy: "From now on, an alien who is in the U.S. temporarily and wants a green card must return to their home country to apply." This forces people already in the country to leave, wait in their home country while the process plays out—which could take months or years—and then apply from abroad. The agency mentions exceptions for "extraordinary circumstances" but provides no definition. It remains unclear whether family ties, employment at a rural hospital, or job loss risk qualify. Case officers will have discretion, but without guidance, applicants face deep uncertainty. Adrian Ma notes the policy is part of the Trump administration's broader push to curb both illegal and legal immigration and signals the issue will likely face legal challenges.
