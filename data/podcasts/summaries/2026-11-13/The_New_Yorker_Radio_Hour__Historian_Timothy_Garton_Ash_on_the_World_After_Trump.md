# The New Yorker Radio Hour — "Historian Timothy Garton Ash on the World After Trump"

**Host:** David Remnick. **Guest:** Timothy Garton Ash (historian, Oxford University; fellow, Hoover Institution, Stanford; author of *Europe in Seven and a Half Chapters*; leading chronicler of Eastern European upheaval and communist collapse).

## TL;DR

- **Liberal hubris after 1989 created vulnerability** — the West believed "mission accomplished" in the early 2000s, failing to prepare for Putin's imperial reassertion, the financial crisis, and China's rise as a competing superpower.
- **Ukraine's maximum viable outcome is independence and EU accession after accepting territorial loss** — Garton Ash estimates roughly one-fifth of Ukraine's territory (Crimea, Eastern Ukraine) is already de facto lost; full restoration and Putin on trial in The Hague is impossible, but sovereign Ukraine joining the EU constitutes a huge Putin defeat.
- **Putin will negotiate only when his generals tell him he's losing** — his current calculus treats the war as a "gigantic meat grinder" he can win through attrition; Western support for Ukraine must increase to shift this calculation.
- **European far-right parties are positioned to consolidate power by end of 2027** — elections in Spain, Germany, France, Italy, and Poland show far-right gains; if multiple Malonies (Italy's Giorgia Meloni model) govern the EU simultaneously, the center of gravity shifts, producing a fundamentally different bloc.
- **NATO trust in Article 5 is permanently damaged** — the security umbrella that enabled European integration since 1945 is now gone; Europe must build independent hard power and reinforce democratic middle-power alliances (Canada, Australia, Japan).
- **Chinese authoritarianism now looks more attractive than Western democracy from the Global South** — from South America and Africa, China's "developmental authoritarianism" and economic performance outshine post-2008 Western dysfunction; the ideological competition is no longer communism vs. fascism, but "Western democratic capitalism failing to deliver."
- **Europe faces an unprecedented triple military, economic, and political attack** — Russia militarily, China economically, and the United States politically; Eastern Europe (Balkans, Hungary, Greece) is being "colonized" economically by Russia, China, and Turkey—Europe shifts from geopolitical actor to geopolitical object.
- **Five far-right governments would reshape the EU's center of gravity** — one Maloney adapts to EU norms; five Malones displace those norms entirely, enabling the "scramble for Europe" by hostile powers and reversing centuries of European agency.

---

**The Failure of Liberal Confidence After the Cold War's End**

Garton Ash attributes the current crisis to what he calls "hubris" in the early 2000s. The West, having witnessed the fall of communism and the collapse of the Soviet Union, convinced itself that history was moving its way and that liberal democracy would advance inexorably. This confidence masked two structural failures. First, Vladimir Putin—who "clearly didn't like what had happened"—launched an "empire striking back," reasserting Russian hegemony and imperial ambitions. Second, the 2008 global financial crisis and subsequent Eurozone crisis exposed deep vulnerabilities in Western neoliberal models: extreme inequality, unsustainable debt, and loss of public faith in liberal institutions. Simultaneously, China rose as a competing superpower, offering an alternative development model.

**China's Developmental Authoritarianism as Ideological Rival**

Garton Ash observes that from the vantage point of the Global South—South America, Africa—China's "developmental authoritarianism" looks more attractive than Western democratic capitalism. The United States has struggled since 2008, producing financial instability, political dysfunction, and visible social breakdown; China, by contrast, has delivered infrastructure, economic growth, and state capacity. The historical analogy with the 1930s breaks down: few now believe Putin's Russia or even Xi Jinping's China represents the future, but many in developing regions believe Western democracy has simply "failed to deliver." This represents a fundamental shift in the ideological competition: neither communism nor fascism dominates thinking, but rather a pragmatic judgment that Western models are broken.

**Ukraine's Realistic Path Forward**

On Ukraine, Garton Ash rejects both the fantasy of a "just peace" and defeatist resignation. A just peace—Ukraine regaining all territory and Putin tried in The Hague—is impossible. However, Ukraine can achieve victory by accepting the de facto loss of approximately one-fifth of its territory (Crimea and Eastern Ukraine already controlled by Russia), establishing itself as a strong, sovereign, independent state on a path into the European Union. This outcome represents a massive defeat for Putin because his imperial worldview requires Ukraine as part of the Russian sphere. The prerequisite is sustained and increased Western support; Putin currently believes he can out-grind Ukraine in a "meat grinder" war. Only when his generals inform him he is actually losing will he negotiate seriously.

**Russian Hybrid Threats and NATO's Eroded Deterrent**

Baltic states, Poland, and other NATO members live under constant escalating Russian hybrid attack—drones loaded with explosives, sabotage, and intelligence operations. Garton Ash notes that Putin might calculate it worthwhile to test NATO's commitment by seizing a small corner of European territory—Svalbard or Narva in Estonia—not to grab vast chunks, but to demonstrate that with Trump in the White House, NATO is "a paper tiger." The fundamental problem is that NATO's Article 5 guarantee has lost credibility; Europeans no longer trust that the United States would actually defend them in a showdown. This trust, which sustained European integration and security from 1945 to 2025, is now "blown."

**The Consolidation of Far-Right Power Across Europe**

Garton Ash describes a "mega cycle of elections" running through the end of 2027: Spain (end of November 2026), Germany (already showing strong Alternative for Germany gains), France (Marine Le Pen positioned to win), Italy, and Poland. The pattern is clear: far-right and anti-liberal nationalist populist parties are advancing. By the end of 2027, Europe could be fundamentally transformed. One Maloney—Italy's Giorgia Meloni, a post-fascist figure who has moderated into mainstream governance—can adapt to EU norms and EU funding requirements. But five Malones simultaneously governing across the continent would shift the EU's center of gravity entirely, producing a continental bloc hostile to liberal internationalism. This dynamic is historically unsurprising (empires do face periodic challenges), but it is not inevitable; if younger Europeans mobilize and broad coalitions form, the elections remain winnable for pro-liberal forces.

**Anti-Semitism, Gaza, and the Complications of Nationalism**

Garton Ash acknowledges Simon Schama's alarm over rising anti-Semitism in Britain, France, and elsewhere as a "canary in the coal mine" for nationalism in its most extreme forms. He distinguishes between two phenomena: old-fashioned European anti-Semitism (particularly on the right and in Poland) and left-wing passion around Gaza and Palestine. The latter, he suggests, would substantially dissipate if there were a significant change in the Israel-Palestine relationship; what would then become visible is "hardcore, very worrying actual anti-Semitism and xenophobia." He does not conflate the two but acknowledges they both present serious risks to liberal democracy.

**Leadership Vacuum and the Case for Liberal Patriotism**

Garton Ash laments the absence of moral and political leadership comparable to Václav Havel, the Czech playwright-dissident who embodied liberal moral authority during the 1989 transitions. To Bertolt Brecht's line—"unhappy the land that has no heroes"—the response is clear: unhappy lands don't need heroes; unhappy lands need them desperately. The current moment needs it. However, Garton Ash identifies some hopeful figures: Peter Magyar in Hungary, Kaya Kallas (European High Representative), Danish PM Mette Frederiksen, and UK PM Andy Burnham. Burnham, in particular, understands that liberals must reclaim emotional connection and patriotic language; the left ceded "the nation" and "emotion" to the right, a catastrophic strategic error. Burnham's approach—a "liberal patriotism"—suggests one way forward.

**Immigration, Integration, and European Identity**

European societies have transformed beyond recognition in 60 years. In the 1970s, approximately one in 20 people had a migration background; now it is one in five or one in four, rising to one in three in younger cohorts and one in two in major cities. Garton Ash argues that Europe's challenge is not whether to accept immigration (the demographic and economic need is real), but whether to manage it effectively and integrate newcomers successfully. The West's error over the past 20–30 years was to allow "possibly too many immigrants, somewhat uncontrolled immigration," while failing to invest in integration. The corrective is reversed: tighter control of immigration flows but much deeper integration of those already present. Britain, despite Brexit, has advantages here; Britishness is already a "baggy identity" encompassing multiple nationalities, and Britain has historically proven relatively successful at integration compared to some Continental models.

**Brexit's Failure and the Long Path to EU Rejoining**

Opinion polls now show a clear British majority regrets Brexit; if voters could wave a wand, most would restore Britain's full EU membership. However, the practical path is very difficult. Rejoining the EU "is an absolutely bloody process," as Garton Ash notes from observing Central and East European accessions; Britain would negotiate from far weaker terms and secure a much worse deal than the original. Politically, the 2029 election will likely see a left bloc (Labour, Lib Dems, Scottish and Welsh nationalists) competing in pro-Europeanism against a right bloc (Conservatives, Reform UK under Nigel Farage) in anti-Europeanism. To move past this and achieve political consensus on rejoining will require five to ten years of shifted opinion and leadership.

**The Breakdown of "The West" and the Rise of Non-Western Power**

Garton Ash articulates a foundational shift: the West, as a geopolitical actor, no longer exists. The United States' trajectory from 2016 to 2026 has destroyed the coherence of what was once called "The West." However, this collapse creates opportunity. If Europe substantially increases defense spending, builds hard power, and strengthens connections with other democratic middle powers (Canada, Australia, Japan), a "reshaped and restructured liberal international order" could emerge when a better American president takes office in January 2029—imagining figures like Pete Buttigieg or John Ossoff.

**The Post-Western World: China and the Limits of Sanctions**

For the first time in approximately 200 years, major non-Western powers acting together or in the same direction possess sufficient collective wealth and power to counterbalance the West. This explains why sanctions on Russia have proven less effective than anticipated. Russia's economy actually performed better than many Western economies for two to three years after 2022, not because sanctions failed, but because Putin had China to provide trade and technology, India to absorb oil, and Turkey, Brazil, and South Africa to maintain economic engagement. The lesson is structural: the non-Western world now has real agency and options. Europe must both increase its own capacity and strengthen ties with other democratic middle powers—Japan, South Korea, Australia—to counter this realignment.

**Taiwan, U.S. Credibility, and the "Gentle Squeeze"**

Garton Ash argues that Xi Jinping, observing the Ukraine war, recognizes the risks of direct military invasion of Taiwan but also understands that "gentle squeezing by economic and political means" will eventually succeed without triggering a credible U.S. response. U.S. allies in the Asia-Pacific—Japan, South Korea, Australia—face the same erosion of security guarantees that Europe does, though they lack Europe's collective scale. This asymmetry creates vulnerability across the democratic alliance system.

**The Structural Case Against Optimism About Italy and the Far-Right Moderation Thesis**

While some commentators point to Giorgia Meloni—describing her as having evolved from a post-fascist to a "post-post-fascist" due to her moderation in office—as evidence that far-right parties can be tamed, Garton Ash is skeptical of this argument at scale. One Maloney, constrained by EU norms and financial dependencies, can adapt. But five Malones simultaneously holding power across the EU shifts the fundamental center of gravity. The analytical question is not whether individual far-right leaders can moderate, but whether multiple far-right governments can reshape the EU's institutional and political norms. Historically, this is not surprising; empires periodically face major challenges to their foundational order.

**The Scramble for Europe: Reversing Centuries of Agency**

The most startling reversal Garton Ash identifies is geopolitical inversion. For centuries, Europe colonized Africa and the world; now Europe itself is being colonized—economically and politically. Russia, China, Turkey, and others are actively acquiring influence and assets across the Balkans, Hungary, Greece, and beyond. Europe, historically a subject of history (an actor shaping events), has become an object of other powers' ambitions. This represents a reversal of the previous 500 years, a return to vulnerability that is "tasting its own medicine."

**The Structural Problems Beneath Democratic Disaffection**

Garton Ash attributes continued Trumpism and far-right ascendance to unresolved structural failures in liberal democracies. Oligarchy is advancing—Elon Musk's influence over elections, corporate dominance of politics, billionaire wealth shaping governance. Affordable housing is in crisis across Europe; young people in major cities cannot rent apartments. Unless liberals address these structural problems—inequality, housing, economic security—that resulted from 30 years of neoliberal policy, they cannot counter the anger fueling right-wing and left-wing insurgencies. The anger at "the world we liberals built" remains overwhelming.

**Putin's 2007 Munich Speech as Turning Point**

Garton Ash reflects on Putin's 2007 Munich Security Conference address as a historical hinge. Putin declared an end to accommodation with the West, pointing to U.S. hypocrisy in Iraq, Afghanistan, and elsewhere, and warning the West to stop "scolding" him. He then executed exactly what he threatened. His critique, though exaggerated and self-serving, exploited real Western hypocrisy—a lever Putin used to justify his own revisionism in world politics. In some measure, Garton Ash concedes, "he was not wrong."

**Conclusion: The Need for Better American Leadership and European Self-Reliance**

The path forward requires two things. First, Europe must urgently increase defense spending, build hard power, deepen integration among democratic middle powers, and cultivate resilience independent of American commitment. Second, the United States must elect a "charismatic internationalist" president in 2029 who can help reconstruct a liberal international order. Without a better American president and without European self-mobilization, the next decade will see liberal democracy further eroded, far-right consolidation across Europe, and a fundamentally reshuffled global order in which the West has become a junior partner rather than a shaping force.
