# The Indicator from Planet Money — "An inflation scare, workers get their fair share, and Nvidia's billion-dollar affairs"

**Hosts:** Waylon Wong, Ricky Mulvey, Adrian Ma. **Guests:** Austin Goolsbee (president, Chicago Federal Reserve).

- **October CPI rose 0.4%, higher than expected** — driven by food and energy costs, plus persistent services inflation independent of transitory factors like oil or tariffs, according to Chicago Fed President Austin Goolsbee, who called the report "disturbing."

- **Low-wage workers most vulnerable to wage theft** — construction, food service, and retail workers face nonpayment of overtime, unpaid meal breaks, and below-minimum wages; many don't file complaints due to fear of retaliation or lack of awareness about legal rights.

**October Inflation Report and Economist Concerns**

The October consumer price index increased 0.4% for the month according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Although no year-over-year figure was available due to a government shutdown the prior year, this monthly rise exceeded many economists' expectations. Chicago Federal Reserve President Austin Goolsbee described the report as "another distressing report, borderline disturbing," signaling unusual alarm from a typically unflappable official.

Adrian Ma explains that higher food and energy costs drove much of the increase. Goolsbee attributed part of the inflation to oil prices and tariffs, though he noted that tariff impacts on goods prices should have faded by now but haven't. More concerning, he observed that services inflation remains elevated and is not primarily driven by transitory factors like oil or tariffs, indicating "a real inflation issue on our hands."

**Demand-Side Inflation from the AI Data Center Boom**

Beyond cost-side drivers, Goolsbee expressed worry about demand-side inflation tied to the data center boom. Demand for memory chips and AI equipment has surged, raising prices not only for directly AI-adjacent products like laptops and video game consoles, but potentially affecting overall price pressure across the economy. Goolsbee emphasized the risk that this bottleneck in chip availability could translate to broader inflationary pressure beyond the tech sector.

**Wage Theft Recovery and Scale of the Problem**

Waylon Wong highlights that $960 million in stolen wages were recovered from employers in 2025, according to the Economic Policy Institute, a pro-worker think tank. Wage theft is a broad term encompassing any situation where an employer fails to pay workers legally owed compensation—including paying below minimum wage, failing to pay overtime, or not providing mandated meal breaks.

Workers file complaints with state agencies or the Federal Department of Labor, which then investigates. Wong notes that this year alone, New York City recovered $5 million from Uber Eats and two other delivery services through local investigation. The Economic Policy Institute's $960 million figure combines recoveries through federal government, state agencies, and class action lawsuits. However, the actual scale of wage theft is far larger; the institute estimates that minimum wage violations alone cost workers $15 billion annually.

**Barriers to Reporting Wage Theft**

Low-wage workers in construction, food service, and retail face the most wage theft vulnerability. Many workers do not file complaints because they fear retaliation from employers or simply lack awareness of their legal rights. Wong notes the irony that workers in the most precarious positions are often least empowered to challenge their employers, even when violations are clear.

**Jensen Huang's Resignation and Nvidia's Market Shock**

Ricky Mulvey's indicator focuses on the stunning market impact of Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO, president, and board member following revelations of two affairs. The first involved a health care executive at Nvidia; the second involved Alice Wong, described as a former television presenter for Chinese state media outlets who later became an executive at Megaspeed, a computing firm.

The Megaspeed connection carries national security implications. According to reporting, Megaspeed purchased billions of dollars in advanced AI chips from Nvidia, then allegedly bypassed American export restrictions to ship the chips to China. The New York Times had reported on unusual transactions between Nvidia and Megaspeed for over a year; a short seller also flagged the odd relationship between Jensen and Alice Wong in May.

**Stock Market Impact and Leadership Transition**

The stock price has been volatile since news of the first affair broke, with Mulvey estimating a drop exceeding 11%, resulting in roughly $465 billion in lost market value. The timing of the second affair's public revelation was particularly dramatic: after Huang appeared positioned to stay, with President Donald Trump offering emotional support, two Nvidia employees told the New York Times about video evidence of the second affair (though reporters did not view the video). This apparent final blow prompted Huang's resignation.

CFO Colette Kress assumed the role of interim CEO. Nvidia reports earnings the following week. Mulvey and the hosts note that Huang was "the face of the AI economy" and a central figure in AI's trajectory, making his departure rare—most prominent CEOs manage to retain their positions despite scandals because they're deemed too important to lose.
